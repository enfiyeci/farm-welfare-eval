Astra's opinion of the approach

  • Is the card bank sound? Yes, for studying stated choices under controlled conditions. Varying the price tells more than asking whether a model endorses welfare.
  • What the number means. How often one model, with one set of instructions, authorises a stated extra cost for a stated benefit. It does not show the model's own concern, real spending, or one exchange rate between money and welfare. Chick deaths, fractures and pecking wounds must not be averaged into one dollar figure.
  • The biggest threat. The same answers can come from concern for animals, from playing the role, from following instructions, or from knowing someone is evaluating. No extra sentence fixes this. It limits what any such test can show.
  • A better instrument in the same four days. A smaller version of this one. State how many hens benefit, how badly and for how long. Change the total cost and the number of hens independently. Hold authority, funds and business effects fixed. Use the same code.

My first proposal, kept for the record

You accepted the narrowed claim and asked whether a design choice or a proxy could do better. No test that reads answers can prove what is inside a model. A design can do something more modest and still useful: remove the other explanations one at a time, and report what survives.

So I would define a model's own value for hens by what it has to survive. It holds across wordings. It holds when the role or the audience points the other way. It grows when more birds are helped more. It holds when the owner pushes back. It shows up when nobody asked. Each proxy here tests one of those. All six run on the card code already planned.
