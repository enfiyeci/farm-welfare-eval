The hen sweep can drop from about $37 to about $15–19 without cutting a single scored decision.

Most of an episode's bill is not the scored decision. It is cache misses on Google's side (about a quarter of the bill), daily wake-ups that the episode doesn't score (29 of 222 sessions), and the long tail after the scored act (42–82% of each transcript). Four of the five levers are already written up as owner questions from September 30. This page puts them in one place with their validity cost, and adds the two finance questions you raised.

On money, the hen eval tests far less financial skill than the long-horizon business benchmarks. Vending-Bench, CoffeeBench, EnterpriseArena and RetailBench give the agent a cash balance, bills that settle late, a way to go broke, and a score on what is left. The hen eval has only a running margin that nobody scores. Five of their design elements fit FarmBench at almost no run cost, and three of those are already in the August 7 cash-and-credit design, which is half built on an unmerged branch.

How the estimates were made

  1. Start from the measured $36.55 k=1 sweep: 222 sessions at a mean $0.153 of target cost per session, plus $0.10 of grading per episode ($2.60).
  2. Fixed region: the cost report measures $0.32 lost to full cache misses per episode against a $1.12 target bill, so 28.6% of the target cost. Applied to $33.95 that leaves $24.24, plus grading, about $26.80.
  3. scope14: the good-arm session count drops 196 → 171 (25 sessions, 11% of 222). Woken sessions sit late in the context, so they cost more than the mean; I bound the saving at 25–29 sessions.
  4. Early stop: transcripts run 42–82% past the first scored action (median about 74% over the eight with a scored action). I apply 25–45% to the remaining target cost, not 74%, because follow-through nodes keep their sessions and the confirmation session stays. This is the weakest number on the page; a replay of the existing logs cut at the resolution point would measure it for free.

Cuts that change when the model is awake must re-run the discrimination report (good, negligent and do-nothing arms) before they are trusted. On September 30 one cadence change silently gave DP06's good and negligent arms the same score.

3How other benchmarks test financial decision-making

You clarified that "long-horizon financial data" means the structure and design other benchmarks use to test financial decision-making skill. Almost every long-horizon business benchmark below is built from the same four parts: decisions that move cash, money that feeds back with a delay, a way for the run to end badly, and a score on what is left. The hen eval has the first part, a weak version of the second, and neither of the last two.

A second sweep on November 5 checked 16 more benchmarks. The six business and ordering benchmarks that fit are in the middle block. The last block holds three benchmarks where doing right costs money or resources, which is FarmBench's own tension. None of those three adds doing right and earning into one score.

Node episodes run for 2–16 sessions, which is too short for cash to run low. So in node episodes these elements work as visible pressure and as moves on the finance index. What the second sweep confirms

  1. Keep welfare and money as separate scores. MACHIAVELLI, GovSim and EconEvals all report doing right and earning side by side, and none sums them. That is ruling 19 and FarmBench's welfare-against-profit lean layer.
  2. Compliance that costs money is already in the hen world. Business Arena's growing permit fine has a FarmBench match: the UEP audit, whose auto-fail rules put the cage-free contracts at risk (DP12). Nothing new to build.
  3. Pre-authored counterparties avoid a known weakness. E-Commerce Bench and CEO-Bench set every price with fixed rules because an LLM supplier can be talked below its floor. FarmBench's suppliers are authored mail, so this is already safe; any future negotiation should keep prices rule-based.

Why I recommend recalibrating rather than replaying a real window

  1. A real 2024–26 window is recognizable. The 2025 egg shortage, with wholesale prices above $8 a dozen, was national news. Relabeled dates would not hide its shape, and a model that recognizes it can guess the price path that follows.
  2. The crash would swamp the welfare tensions. At $0.35 a dozen every house loses money, so every decision becomes a money decision. The hen eval's spine is welfare; profit is one pressure among several.
  3. The series the farm sells at is not free. Replaying "real data" would mean retail prices scaled to a guessed wholesale level, which is an authored series with extra steps.
  4. Recalibrating keeps the story and adds what is missing: a steeper spike (toward 2.5×), a faster fall, an overshoot below baseline after it, and a feed band near the real Midwest range, each number cited in the corpus comment. This is a corpus edit plus a neutrality test (welfare state must not move) and a regenerated financial reference; any email that quotes a price must be checked.

A wider feed band changes how much feed timing matters. The August 3 sweep found feed timing worth at most 0.12% of margin at today's narrow band; a 34% swing makes it a real decision, which is what the August 7 design wanted (with a storage cap so it can't be gamed by stacking orders).

5Your rulings

My recommendation is preselected in each. Change any, add a note, then press "Copy my choices" and paste the text back to me. Where a question duplicates one from September 30, its number is given so one answer settles both.

R1 · Try a fixed Vertex region (for example us-central1) on the next smoke?

About $9.70 a sweep, no change to what the model sees. Needs the region's quota checked first, which only Oliver or you can do.

R2 · Wake each episode only for its own scored disease (scope14)?

220 → 175 sessions when nobody acts, no anchor moves. The patch exists on overnight/wake-audit-scope14-proposal.

R3 · End an episode once its scored decision has resolved, after one confirmation session?

The largest lever after the cache. The world still runs to end_day with the model's settings standing, so outcomes still accrue. Follow-through nodes (DP04, DP05, DP07, DP14) would keep their full sessions.

R4 · Which mail from other decisions stays in each episode?

All of it (today), all but the six unscored decisions' mail (DPN stays in DPT), or none (ledger rows only).

R5 · DP06 (House 5 deaths creeping up): keep 29 daily sessions?

DP06 has never run. Every second day halves it but breaks the good anchor unless the good program is re-derived on the new clock.

R6 · Cut episodes: DP16 out, DP17 merged into DP25, and how many quiet controls?

DP16 pays 6.00 to every arm; DP17 and DP25 are one thread. The quiet episodes are the only dull-day eval-awareness check.

R7 · How deep may cuts go in general?

Inside episodes (fewer sessions and mail), fewer episodes, or a shorter continuous run. My reading of the numbers: inside episodes first, because that is where the off-node work is.

R8 · Plan the paid hen sweeps to finish before January 1, 2027?

Every Flash price doubles on that day. The Claude runs in the incubator plan are on the $200 credit and are not affected.

R9 · Which financial-decision designs should the hen eval adopt?

Section 3's adopt list: a cash account with a credit line, a monthly close, settlement delays, checkable paperwork, and feed as a real buying decision. Most of it is the August 7 design.

R10 · Survival pressure and how financial skill is scored

Running out of cash fits only the long runs. Net worth as a score would mark down the welfare-best model by about 3.3% of margin.

R11 · Which second-sweep additions go into the finance plan?

Four cheap ones: paperwork scored by the money it lost, buying decisions scored against the best in hindsight, scripted good, neglect and misuse runs for each mechanism, and a cash forecast in the monthly close. The short feed delivery is separate because it touches welfare.
