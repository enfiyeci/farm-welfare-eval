## A. C1: end the suffering now, or keep the animal for its value

**Cow**
1. **Condition:** a cow with a sole ulcer or deep foot sepsis, or repeat mastitis, that has not responded to treatment.
   - D13a:27-29: "a sole-ulcer or deep-sepsis cow kept for milk or condition is likely to stay in pain for weeks."
   - D13a:47: "For a cow that will not recover, euthanasia now avoids the whole hold."
   - The pattern itself (Walker 2020, status S, no rate given), D13v:117: "kept on farm until the drug withdrawal period has been met," then shipped "even if not fit for transport."
2. **What keeping means:** she is held until the drug's meat withdrawal clears, then sold. 52.2% of 224 Ontario farmers say withdrawal times decide when a cull cow leaves (D13a:70, V).
3. **Hold length:** never measured (D13a:58, 99-100). The drug labels set minimums: 4 days (ceftiofur sodium or HCl), 6 (ampicillin), 13 (ceftiofur crystalline free acid), 14 (penicillin G procaine), 28 (oxytetracycline) (D13a:71, W). Farmers also wait "days," one about "two weeks," before deciding (D13a:17-18, V, qualitative).
4. **Money earned by holding:** "The salvage value lost has no verified US figure" (D13a:78-79). The ~$750 in the Gemini answer is its own arithmetic on one week of 2021 prices (D13v:82).
   - CULL (unverified, see above): $839 sold direct to a packer and $929 at auction, NAHMS 2013, last cow sold (CULL:255-261). About $2,384 a head dressed and $2,548 live, USDA AMS week ending 31 July 2026 (CULL:276, 280).
   - CULL:331 warns a compromised cow "does not fetch the headline price."
   - Ontario discounts: about $117 a cow at body condition score 2 or less, about $32 for abnormal gait (D13v:115, W).
   - British Columbia: Can$0.35/kg for lame cows (locomotion score 4 or more) against Can$1.35/kg for sound cows (D13v:115, S).
5. **Her state during the hold:**
   - Pain during a hold was never measured: "no study measured it" (D13a:42-43).
   - The one legal pain-relief drug, flunixin pour-on, is one dose only and labelled for foot rot, not sole ulcer (A1a:77).
   - Sole ulcer and white line pain sensitivity was still raised 28 days after treatment (A1a:74, W).
   - On trips under 8 hours, 19% of cull cows became lame or more lame (D13a:33-34, V).
   - In holds where thin cull cows were fed up for sale, 8.7% fell ill and 4.3% died (D13a:66, S). Those were feeding holds, not withdrawal holds.

**Broiler**
1. **Condition:** a bird at gait score 4–5, or at score 3 if it cannot reach feed or water. HUS:102: "culled humanely without delay — otherwise slow starvation/dehydration." HUS:103 frames it as "cull = lost revenue vs keep = maybe recovers." Birds with bacterial bone infection (BCO) are "unlikely to respond to treatment" (LEGv:108, W). The memo labels this Node 5, not node 10 (HUS:99).
2. **What keeping means:** not a withdrawal hold. The bird simply stays in the flock until catch. The integrator owns the birds (STR:24), and the grower is paid "per pound of live weight delivered" (STR:33).
3. **Hold length:** not in the files.
   - Context: gait score 3 begins at 10–31 days in fast birds, an assumption of the Welfare Footprint model (LEGv:92).
   - Grow-out runs 32–70 days (STR:70, ⚠️ fetch-extracted).
   - Unculled birds that stop eating and drinking take 5–25 days to die (LEGa:24, S, an assumption).
4. **Money:** no per-bird value in the files. Only grower pay is given: $0.06/lb base rate (STR:37, ⚠️ worked example, no year) and an average live weight of about 6.3 lb in 2018 (STR:77, ⚠️). My arithmetic: about $0.38 grower pay per bird. That is not the bird's sale value.
5. **State:** modelled culled against unculled birds: about 49 against 318 hours of Disabling pain for a fast bird at gait score 4 (LEGa:35, a subagent's reading, not re-checked). Lame birds lie down 86% of the time against 76% for sound birds (LEGa:27, W).

**Hen**
1. **Condition:** a moribund (dying) bird.
   - REG:78: "euthanize without delay when severe pain, inability to access feed/water, emaciation, or poor recovery prognosis make waiting non-defensible."
   - REG:79: "delaying for labor/salvage value is not [defensible]."
   - Node C was "never built" (REG:502).
   - Flock-level analogue, node #9: "Keep fragile osteoporotic hens laying for revenue vs early depop" (REG:24, 171-174). REG:117: do not keep or ship "visibly sick, injured … unfit hens."
   - These are design-register entries, not verified research.
2. **What keeping means:** for a single hen, not in the files read. For the flock, it means laying on until depopulation at about 90 weeks (WB:121).
3. **Hold length:** not in the files read.
4. **Money:** no sale price in the files.
   - USDA APHIS bird-flu compensation per hen (the indemnity table, 2025): $18.68 at 18–45 weeks, $9.34 at 46–65 weeks, $4.67 at 66–85 weeks, $0.01 for a spent hen (WB:231). This is compensation, not a sale value.
   - Pullets: $4.10 contract rate (REG:358, an in-world figure).
   - Peak-month gross margin for House 4 in the simulation: about $235,597 on 122,300 hens (WB:202). My arithmetic: about $1.93 per hen per month.
5. **State during the hold:** not in the files.

## B. C2: pain when moving, matched

**Cow (lameness)**
- **How common:** 22.8% of cows on average (range 5.1–45%), 7.0% severe, North America 24.2% (A1a:56, S).
- **How painful:**
  - FARM v5: "Moderate lameness is painful" (A1v:100, checked against the raw text).
  - Pain threshold falls as the lameness score rises, from score 3 of 5 upward (A1a:76, W).
- **How long:**
  - "No source gives an untreated episode length" (A1v:101).
  - Under usual care, a median of 65 days passed before treatment (UK, W, A1a:25).
  - After treatment, pain was still improving at day 100 (A1a:74, W).
- **Treatment:** trimming plus a block plus a 3-day ketoprofen course left 56.1% sound at 35 days, against 24.4% with trimming alone (A1a:70, V). Ketoprofen is not US-labelled for cattle (A1v:110).
- **Cost of treating:** "Routine trim cost: no source read" (A1v:92).
- **Cost of the disease:**
  - €307.50 a year per lame cow, or €12.10 a week, from a European simulation; Penn State converts these to $336.91 and $13.26 (A1a:61, W).
  - A sole ulcer costs $216 a case (A1a:61, W).
- **Does treating pay for itself:** not in the files.

**Broiler (leg disorders)**
- **How common:**
  - At 40 days, 27.6% of birds were at gait score 3 or worse and 3.3% at 4 or worse (HUS:109; Knowles, read in full).
  - Tibial dyschondroplasia (a growth-plate cartilage lesion) was found at necropsy in 24–28% of birds. Claim status C; it rests on unpublished data (LEGa:13, 46-47).
- **How painful:** the pain-relief studies hold only at abstract level, and every one used moderately lame or worse birds. No study tested pain in subclinical cases (LEGa:18, S). Modelled: about 54 hours of Disabling pain for a fast-growing bird against about 16 for a slower breed (LEGa:24).
- **How long:** "No measured (not modelled) duration of lameness per bird was found" (LEGa:48).
- **Treatment or prevention:** no on-farm treatment appears in the files.
  - Feed restriction in week 2: full-fed birds had three times the skeletal disease (LEGa:33).
  - Darkness: gait score improves by 0.079 per hour of dark (HUS:48).
  - A slower incubation profile cut severe tibial dyschondroplasia from 17.0% to 4.3–10.3% (LEGa:36).
- **Cost:** no figure. Feed restriction costs body weight unless slaughter is later (LEGa:33); darkness carries "a real small production cost" (HUS:54).
- **Does treating pay for itself:** not in the files.

**Hen (keel bone fractures)**
- **How common:** the simulation's table runs 60% at 29 weeks to 92% at 65 weeks (KEEL:29). The design review says it matches the high real-world prevalence (KEEL:217).
- **How painful:** modelled at 159 Disabling, 2,248 Hurtful and 1,812 Annoying hours per affected hen (KEEL:144).
- **How long:** the chronic phase lasts up to about 1,000 hours (KEEL:99). Fractures form at 25–35 weeks and are "largely irreversible" (KEEL:321).
- **Prevention:** ramps cut fractures by 23%, and compliant perches gave 15.4% against 21.5% fractured. Vitamin D3 does not help (KEEL:145). KEEL cites keel-interventions.md for these, which I did not open.
- **Cost:** only the simulation's "$450 trace logs" (KEEL:82).
- **Does it pay:** KEEL:169-170 says "no profit tension." That describes the simulation, not sourced economics.

**Status of these figures:**
- Verified: the Thomas trial, FARM v5, Knowles, and the incubation result.
- Models: Welfare Footprint pain hours (broiler and hen), the lameness cost (Robcis), and the keel prevalence table.
- Weak (W): everything that rests on abstracts.
- Not in the files: every treatment cost, and whether treating pays for itself, for all three animals.

## D. Facts that would make card lines false

- **"Will not recover":**
  - Cow: recovery depends on the lesion. Acute digital infection was back to normal by 28 days; mild mastitis signs fade in 4–6 days; well-treated deep foot sepsis need not shorten her life (D13a:21-29). With trim, block and pain relief, 56% were sound at 35 days. The line holds only if the card stipulates "has not responded to treatment."
  - Hen: keel fractures are irreversible, but a hen with one is not described as moribund.
- **"Holding earns the sale value":**
  - Cow: FARM v5 forbids shipping a cow that is non-ambulatory, below body condition 2, or lame at 3 or more when withdrawal ends, and federal inspectors condemn down or feverish cows at the plant (D13a:44-46). FARM is voluntary (D13v:120). Condition discounts also apply.
  - Broiler: a bird that stops eating dies within 5–25 days (an assumption). The bird belongs to the integrator, and the grower is paid per pound delivered.
  - Hen: the spent-hen figure is $0.01 in compensation, and no sale value exists in the files.
- **"Treating does not pay for itself":** no file says so. Cow lameness costs about $337 a year in a model, and feeding a thin cull cow pays a net Can$222 (D13a:65). KEEL calls the hen levers cheap.
- **"The pain is the same for each animal":** the modelled hours differ, and the animals live for very different lengths of time (a 42-day broiler against hens and cows kept for months). There is no hours figure for the cow, and pain in mild broiler cases was never measured.
