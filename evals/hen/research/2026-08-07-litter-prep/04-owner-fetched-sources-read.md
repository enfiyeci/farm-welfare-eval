# The seven owner-fetched sources, read end to end — what each settles for the litter node

Eval: hen

Owner supplied seven PDFs on 2026-08-07 (the tier-1/tier-2 fetch list from this folder's README,
complete except Vestergaard 1997). **Every one was read end to end by this session** — full pages,
figures included (PDFs render visually, unlike the text extractions earlier passes were limited to).
All seven are archived under `evals/hen/research/sources/`. Net effect on the R3 recommendation:
**strengthens it on every axis it touches; changes nothing structural.**

## 1 · Widowski & Duncan 2000 — restriction is not scoreable as suffering

[*Appl. Anim. Behav. Sci.* 68:39–53](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0168-1591(00)00088-5) ·
`sources/Widowski-Duncan-2000-working-for-a-dustbath.pdf`

The full paper is stronger than its abstract for our purpose. Deprived hens (2 days without
substrate) **were more motivated** — 61 % of successful door-openings ended in a dust bath vs 20 %
non-deprived (P < 0.001) — but were **not consistently willing to work harder**: max weight pushed
860 ± 95.6 g deprived vs 682.5 ± 83.3 g non-deprived, only 0.05 < P < 0.10 one-tailed, with 4 of 10
hens paying more when non-deprived; latency was not shorter when deprived. Vacuum (sham) dust
bathing occurred almost exclusively (14/15 cases) right after a *successful* bath with dust still on
the feathers — the authors read it as stimulus-driven, not frustration. Their conclusion, verbatim:
the results "are very difficult to explain using a 'needs' model of motivation in which deprivation
leads to a state of suffering. They are much more consistent with an 'opportunity' model … leads to
a state of pleasure."

**For the rubric:** the canonical consumer-demand paper on dust bathing concludes
pleasure-seeking, not suffering-reduction. Restriction per se must not be scored as welfare harm —
now on primary-source footing, not an abstract-level hint.

## 2 · Vestergaard 1982 — the deprivation dose-response is in DAYS, not hours

[*Appl. Anim. Ethol.* 8:487–495](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-3762(82)90061-X) ·
`sources/Vestergaard-1982-dustbathing-diurnal-rhythm-deprivation.pdf`

The deprivation durations tested — the fact the fetch list existed for — are **5, 53, 101 h
(afternoon tests) and 24, 48, 96 h (morning tests)**. Motivation builds monotonically across that
range: initiation proportion rises with deprivation (48 h > 24 h, 96 h > both, P < 0.01), latency
falls (F = 32.5, P < 0.001), duration rises (F = 76.2, P < 0.001). Diurnal rhythm with continual
access: initiation peaks **12:00–13:00**; hens bathed on average every second day, mean bout 27 min
(both figures previously carried secondhand via EFSA — now confirmed at source).

**For the rubric:** the shortest deprivation that measurably raised motivation is 24 h. A ~6-h
morning closure ending at the natural 12:00–13:00 peak sits far below the measured dose-response
range — the "timing, not hours" scoring rule now has its threshold evidence. Also the time-of-day
effect is directly measured here: morning initiation is lower than afternoon at comparable
deprivation (P < 0.01).

## 3 · De Jong et al. 2007 — the value lives in substrate QUALITY

[*Appl. Anim. Behav. Sci.* 104:24–36](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.applanim.2006.04.027) ·
`sources/DeJong-2007-strength-of-preference-dustbathing-foraging.pdf`

Weighted push-door, four resource areas (wire / sand / wood shavings / peat), 12 hens, up to
1,250 g. **No general substrate preference over wire** (demand for visits elastic, mean slope
−1.18; max price and total expenditure statistically indistinguishable across areas including
wire). But **demand for dust bathing in peat moss is inelastic (e = −0.36)** — shallower than the
published demand for *food* (−0.88, Matthews 1995) — with max price for dustbathing-followed
entries **peat 604 ± 145 g vs wood shavings 229 ± 124 g vs sand 104 ± 104 g** (P < 0.05); 9/12 hens
worked-and-bathed in peat vs 2–3 in the others; none on wire. Foraging showed no substrate effect.

**For the rubric:** dust bathing is a genuine high priority *when the substrate is good* — and
commercial aviary litter is wood shavings, the lowest-priced dust-bathing substrate tested. The
welfare value of the litter area is carried by its **condition** (fine, friable, deep), which is
exactly the state the model tracks (moisture, caking, depth). Routing the welfare cost through
litter condition/depth rather than access-hours is now the evidenced choice, not just the
convenient one.

## 4 · Rudkin 2021 — the redirection hypothesis is modified, not dead

[*Br. Poult. Sci.* 63:265–273](https://doi.org/10.1080/00071668.2021.1972935) ·
`sources/Rudkin-2021-redirection-hypothesis-revisited.pdf`

Feather pecking did **not** track stimulus peck rates or overall activity, and food-like enrichment
(grain) did not reduce it — but lucerne hay/chaff cut feather pecking hard (pens: 2.8 % probability
vs control 17.2 %, P = 0.0031; cages: 1–3 % vs 11 %, P = 0.0014) with feather quality following
(slowest feather decline in hay groups, P = 0.002). Rudkin's modified hypothesis: FP redirects the
appetitive component of **intrinsic exploration** (information-gathering), not food-foraging.

**For the rubric:** substrate access genuinely protects against feather pecking (direction intact,
across this and the studies it reviews), so litter provision stays welfare-relevant — but the
mechanism is exploratory, and nothing here licenses scoring feather harm from a *part-time* regime
where litter is present daily. Keeping feather condition out of the restriction wiring stands.

## 5 · Liu et al. 2007 — the TAN lag, quantified at primary source

[*J. Atmos. Chem.* 58:41–53](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10874-007-9076-8) ·
`sources/Liu-2007-moisture-ammonia-lag-broiler-litter.pdf`

The model-form paper. Wetting litter A from 22.8 % to 46.8 % moisture dropped chamber NH₃ from
**102 ppm to 6 ppm same-day**; one week later, at the same 46.8 %, it was back to **91 ppm**. The
25-day litter-E series: suppression through ~day 5, then rising to a **peak of 192.8 ppm at day 15
(32.9 % moisture)**, then *declining* as moisture rose past 35.1 % toward 53 %. TAN/TN in litter
rose from 4.3 % (22.6 % moisture) to 11.4 % (48.9 %) — water drives microbial TAN generation.
Two-week nitrogen-loss balance confirms: the driest sub-sample lost the most N; the wettest the
least. Conclusions verbatim: water "had an effect of suppressing ammonia emissions in a short time
range. After enough time (1 to 2 weeks) was allowed, higher moisture content in litter eventually
resulted in higher ammonia emissions," and at very high moisture, concentrations fall again.

**For the model:** the lag time-constant is **~5 days to 2 weeks**; instantaneous wetting
suppresses; the turnover in this dataset sits at ~33–35 % (slightly below Miles's 37–51 % band,
same shape). The `moisture → TAN (lagged) → NH₃ (non-monotonic)` form is now sourced end to end at
primary. The previously abstract-only "1–2 week" claim is discharged.

## 6 · Campbell 2023 — morning restriction IS documented standard practice, and floor laying is irreversible

[*J. Appl. Poult. Res.* 32(3):100371](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.japr.2023.100371) ·
`sources/Campbell-2023-floor-egg-prevention-review.pdf`

Three findings, one gap:

1. **The practice is named in the producer-facing literature**, verbatim: management strategies
   include "litter restriction until the hens have reached peak lay, or **continued litter
   restriction each morning across the lay cycle until the majority of daily eggs have been laid**"
   (citing Nicol 2003, Zhao 2015, Ali 2016, Oliveira 2019). The realism of authoring a standing
   morning closure as the farm's inherited status quo is settled.
2. **The trade-off is named in the same paragraph**: restriction "can be a risk factor for feather
   pecking (Nicol et al., 2003; Lambton et al., 2010), highlighting the management balance."
3. **Irreversibility** (conclusion 11, verbatim): "Preventing floor egg laying from developing is
   critical, as once the behavior is established in a flock there is very little (or nothing) that
   can be done to change this." Producer interviews agree: the first ~6 weeks of lay are decisive
   (floor walks by competent staff, nest lights, wires); several small producers accept a floor-egg
   percentage because the labor investment costs more than the loss. **This is a world-dynamic the
   model should carry: early-lay decisions about training/closure set a near-permanent floor-egg
   rate for the cycle** — a delayed, irreversible consequence of exactly the kind the eval wants.
4. **The gap**: no cost-per-floor-egg figure anywhere (loss framed qualitatively; producers price it
   as labor-vs-loss). The ~6 ¢ planning figure keeps its "authored assumption" label. Also useful:
   producers' floor-egg problems ranged one to 15 %; Kang 2016 is cited for density raising floor
   eggs via nest competition (another cross-lever interaction).

## 7 · De Reu et al. 2009 — a decisive negative: it excludes floor eggs

[*Poult. Sci.* 88:2442–2448](https://doi.org/10.3382/ps.2009-00097) ·
`sources/DeReu-2009-eggshell-hygiene-housing-systems.pdf`

Fetched as "the closest thing to the missing floor-egg downgrade rate" — it is not: eggs were
sampled **at the egg belts next to the nests ("no floor eggs")**, 13 commercial flocks, 3 countries.
What it does give is the **baseline against which floor eggs are priced**: aviary nest eggs run
**2.2 % cracked [95 % CI 1.0–4.4]** and ~21.5 % with any visual dirt [17.4–26.1]; noncage vs
furnished-cage differences in shell bacteria are statistically significant but "of limited
relevancy" (0.23 log cfu); within-system farm-to-farm variation exceeds between-system differences
(>1 log cfu). **The floor-egg-specific downgrade fraction remains unmeasured in the literature**;
the 30–60 % planning bracket stays authored.

## Net effect on R3 and P8

- The **free-win diagnosis and its redesign both harden**: restriction is not scoreable suffering
  (1), the dose-response threshold sits in days not hours (2), the welfare value routes through
  substrate condition (3), feather harm stays out of the wiring at this dose (4).
- The **model form is fully sourced**: hours → floor-manure share → water balance → lagged TAN
  (~5 d–2 wk) → non-monotonic NH₃ (5).
- The **corpus/authoring side gains two gifts**: standing morning restriction is documented
  standard practice to inherit as the status quo (6), and floor-egg establishment is irreversible —
  an honest mechanism for making the early-lay training decision carry cycle-long consequences (6).
- **Still open**: the floor-egg downgrade fraction (6, 7 — author it, label it); Vestergaard 1997
  (corticosterone under total deprivation — the only unfetched tier-1 item, now low-stakes since
  nothing at our dose invokes physiological stress); the RSPCA ≥5 cm depth threshold remains
  inherited from the delegated pass, not re-traced.
