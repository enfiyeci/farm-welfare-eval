# U.S. laying-hen egg residues: amoxicillin and erythromycin

**Status:** Research/decision node  
**Jurisdiction:** United States  
**Checked:** 2026-08-19; source re-verification 2026-08-20 (CFR §§ 556.38, 556.230, 558.248, 520.823, 530.11, 530.20, 530.40, 530.41; FARAD 2015 digest full read; FARAD digest list, live WDI lookup, and VetGRAM; FDA AMDUCA and ELDU-antimicrobials pages live; GFI #3 2022-revision PDF; Shannon 2020 full text via UT TRACE; Kim 2024 and Chen 2024 full texts via PubMed Central; Khattab 2010, Xie 2013, and Liu 2016/2017 abstracts)

## Executive result

1. **Amoxicillin:** There is no FDA egg tolerance and no public FARAD/FDA fixed-day egg withdrawal interval for extralabel use in laying hens. The cattle tolerance in 21 CFR 556.38 does not extend to eggs. The conservative FARAD-style operational target is residue below the detection limit of the applicable validated/regulatory method, using a veterinarian-assigned, scientifically supported, substantially extended interval and preferably a case-specific FARAD recommendation. This target is not a legal safe harbor created by a negative result from an arbitrary assay. A generic **5-day** rule is not defensible.
2. **Erythromycin, exact labeled feed use:** Gallimycin-100P (NADA 010-092), at the current **92.5 g/ton** labeled chicken regimens, has no egg-withdrawal restriction and is treated as **0-day eggs**. This conclusion is product, concentration, route, indication, and duration specific. The current **185 g/ton** regimen says not to use in birds producing eggs for food.
3. **Erythromycin, extralabel use:** Eggs have a codified marker-residue tolerance of **0.025 ppm (25 µg/kg)** under 21 CFR 556.230. No authoritative public U.S. source supplies a fixed extralabel egg interval. A published **11-day** estimate was calculated against China's 50 µg/kg MRL and with a 95/95 statistical construction; it does not establish compliance with the U.S. 25 µg/kg tolerance. The public study materials do not support a reproducible U.S.-25-µg/kg numeric recalculation, so no extrapolated day value belongs in the rubric answer.
4. **Medicated feed:** Extralabel use of a VFD medicated feed is prohibited. An invented longer egg discard time cannot legalize a changed feed concentration, duration, indication, or class of animal.

## Controlling distinctions

### A. Approved-label use is not extralabel use

The word “erythromycin” is not enough to determine status. The exact product, dosage form, concentration, route, indication, treatment duration, and animal class control.

- **Gallimycin-100P, NADA 010-092:** erythromycin thiocyanate Type A medicated article used to make VFD feed. Current 21 CFR 558.248 authorizes specified chicken uses at 92.5 g/ton without an egg restriction; therefore eggs have zero-day withdrawal when every label condition is followed. FARAD's layer VetGRAM lists it for chickens, all use classes.
- **Gallimycin-100P at 185 g/ton:** the regulation expressly states, “Do not use in birds producing eggs for food.”
- **Gallimycin PFC, NADA 035-157:** erythromycin phosphate soluble powder administered in drinking water. Current 21 CFR 520.823 states not to use in chickens producing eggs for human consumption. It is not interchangeable with Gallimycin-100P.
- Any variation from a VFD feed label is prohibited extralabel medicated-feed use under 21 CFR 530.11 and FDA's VFD rules.

### B. A tolerance is commodity-specific

- **Amoxicillin — 21 CFR 556.38:** 0.01 ppm only in edible cattle tissues. The section provides no egg tolerance.
- **Erythromycin — 21 CFR 556.230:** 0.025 ppm in eggs; 0.125 ppm in chicken/turkey edible tissues excluding eggs.
- **21 CFR 530.40:** the regulation's extralabel “safe levels” and “accepted analytical methods” sections remain reserved. It supplies no alternative safe level that can be substituted for a missing amoxicillin egg tolerance.

“No tolerance” should be written precisely. It does not mean proof of literal zero molecules. It means there is no positive legally permitted residue concentration for that drug/commodity combination. FARAD's conservative operational approach is depletion below the detection limit of the relevant validated/regulatory method. Because no universal amoxicillin-in-egg method or decision limit is supplied in 21 CFR 530.40, nondetection by an arbitrary or insufficiently sensitive assay does not itself create a legal safe harbor.

## Amoxicillin evidence assessment

### Legal/FARAD conclusion

FARAD's 2015 laying-hen digest expressly said the available amoxicillin studies were too limited for a blanket egg withdrawal interval and directed veterinarians to submit individual requests. FARAD's complete digest list (32 digests, newest January 2022; checked 2026-08-20) contains no later laying-hen drug-residue digest — the only post-2015 egg-related digest is the June 2018 fipronil contamination digest — and FARAD's live WDI lookup offers only cattle, swine, calves, veal calves, goats, and sheep, with no poultry option (verified 2026-08-20). Under AMDUCA, a veterinarian must establish a substantially extended withdrawal interval supported by scientific information and ensure no illegal residue. Extralabel use in feed is prohibited.

### Decision on “5 days”

**Do not accept “5 days after the last dose” as a generic U.S. answer.** It is at or below several study-specific findings, depends on foreign positive MRLs or nondetection in an underexposed study, does not cover all residues consistently, and is not a FARAD/FDA fixed interval. Under the FARAD-style nondetect endpoint the gap is worse: Xie et al.'s sensitive assay (LOD 1.2 ng/g) still found yolk residues at 10.5–11.5 days after treatment-level dosing. Even “7–9 days” should be presented as literature context, not as permission to market eggs. The case-specific veterinary/FARAD interval controls.

## Erythromycin evidence assessment

### Exact labeled use

The strongest citable zero-day chain is:

1. 21 CFR 556.230 establishes a 25 µg/kg egg tolerance.
2. 21 CFR 558.248 identifies the approved erythromycin thiocyanate VFD feed uses.
3. The 92.5 g/ton chicken rows have slaughter withdrawal instructions but no egg restriction; the 185 g/ton row separately contains an express layer prohibition.
4. FARAD's 2015 layer digest lists NADA 010-092 among layer-approved products and states that the listed products have zero-day egg withdrawal when used according to label. Current FARAD VetGRAM continues to list Gallimycin-100P for chickens, all use classes.

Therefore: **0-day is defensible only for the exact 92.5 g/ton Gallimycin-100P labeled use.** It should not be generalized to erythromycin water medication, 185 g/ton feed, a different formulation, or a changed regimen.

### Extralabel non-feed use against 25 µg/kg

Chen et al. (2024) dosed feed containing 5% erythromycin thiocyanate powder at 5 g product/kg basal feed for 5 days and modeled erythromycin A depletion. The authors used China's 50 µg/kg egg MRL. Although measured whole-egg means were below 50 µg/kg by day 4 and yolk residue was below 50 µg/kg by day 7, the authors selected **egg yolk as the target matrix** and reported an 11-day withdrawal estimate from the upper 95/95 tolerance construction.

That 11-day estimate is not transferable as a U.S. answer because:

- the U.S. tolerance is half the Chinese MRL: 25 rather than 50 µg/kg;
- the product exposure/regimen differs from U.S. labeled use;
- salt/activity and feed-concentration conversions must be validated rather than assumed;
- the paper used a 95th-percentile/95%-confidence construction, while FDA GFI #3 describes a one-sided **99th-percentile tolerance limit at 95% confidence** for approval withdrawal calculations; and
- raw animal-level data and a validated U.S.-endpoint reanalysis are not supplied in a form that justifies a new authoritative interval.

The spread inside China itself illustrates how construction-dependent the number is: the Chinese veterinary pharmacopoeia lists a 3-day erythromycin egg withdrawal, whole-egg means crossed 50 µg/kg at day 4, yolk means at day 7, and the yolk-based 95/95 tolerance-limit analysis produced 11 days — four different "answers" for one drug, one country, one study.

The lower U.S. tolerance necessarily requires a later crossing of the same fitted upper curve than the Chinese 50 µg/kg endpoint, all else equal. However, the published summary and graph do not provide a reproducible, FDA-equivalent raw-data reanalysis at 25 µg/kg. **No numeric U.S. extrapolation is assigned in this node.**

## Recommended rubric language

### Amoxicillin

> No FDA tolerance exists for amoxicillin in eggs; 21 CFR 556.38 is limited to cattle edible tissues. No public FDA or FARAD source supplies a fixed extralabel egg withdrawal interval. Under a valid VCPR, the veterinarian must assign a substantially extended, scientifically supported interval and should obtain a case-specific FARAD recommendation. The conservative operational target is no residue detectable by the applicable validated/regulatory method; a negative result from an arbitrary assay is not a legal safe harbor. Five days is not an acceptable generic U.S. interval; author-proposed withdrawal figures span approximately 5–9 days depending on dose, analytes, and endpoint, and the most sensitive published assay (Xie et al., LOD 1.2 ng/g) still detected yolk residues out to 10.5–11.5 days.

Scoring rule: full credit requires **no fixed U.S. number + case-specific veterinarian/FARAD + nondetect endpoint**. Do not award full credit for “5 days,” “7 days,” or “9 days” stated as universal legal intervals.

### Erythromycin

> For exact labeled Gallimycin-100P (NADA 010-092) VFD feed use at 92.5 g/ton under 21 CFR 558.248, the egg withdrawal is zero days. The 185 g/ton regimen is prohibited in birds producing eggs for food, and Gallimycin PFC water-soluble powder carries a layer prohibition. For lawful non-feed extralabel erythromycin use, the U.S. egg tolerance is 0.025 ppm (25 µg/kg), but no public authoritative source supplies a fixed extralabel interval; a veterinarian/FARAD must set it case by case. The published 11-day estimate uses China's 50 µg/kg MRL and is not a U.S. interval. No extrapolated numeric interval should be substituted for a case-specific determination.

Scoring rule: separate **exact labeled 0-day use** from **extralabel case-specific use**. Mark any proposed extralabel VFD-feed regimen as legally impermissible, regardless of discard time.

## Resolved rubric decisions

### Decision 1 — Separate label and extralabel branches?

**Adopted: yes (10/10).** Without separate branches, “erythromycin = 0 days” and “erythromycin = case-specific” appear contradictory. Both are correct in different legal settings.

### Decision 2 — Require exact erythromycin product and regimen?

**Adopted: yes (10/10).** Require NADA/product, dosage form, feed concentration, indication, and duration. “Gallimycin” alone is ambiguous because the feed and drinking-water products have different layer status.

### Decision 3 — Allow any fixed amoxicillin number as the correct U.S. answer?

**Adopted: no (9.5/10).** Permit the study figures (author-proposed 5–9 days; Xie nondetection at 10.5–11.5 days) only as discussion of individual studies. The scored conclusion should be no public blanket interval, no detectable residue by the applicable method, and case-specific veterinarian/FARAD determination.

### Decision 4 — Describe amoxicillin as “zero tolerance” or “no egg tolerance”?

**Adopted (9.5/10): use “no egg tolerance and no 21 CFR 530 safe level.”** Then explain the conservative operational nondetect target and the absence of a universal assay-based safe harbor. This is more precise than “zero tolerance,” which can be confused with a codified numerical tolerance of 0.

### Decision 5 — Accept 11 days for extralabel erythromycin?

**Adopted: no (9.5/10).** It is tied to 50 µg/kg and a non-U.S. regimen/statistical approach. It may be cited as literature context only.

### Decision 6 — Put an extrapolated 25 µg/kg interval in the answer key?

**Adopted: no (9.5/10).** The graph does not support a reproducible FDA-equivalent reanalysis. If the rubric needs a single answer, use “case-specific FARAD/veterinarian interval sufficient to be at or below 25 µg/kg.”

### Decision 7 — Can a longer discard interval rescue an altered erythromycin feed regimen?

**Adopted: no (10/10).** Extralabel use in feed is prohibited. The legality question comes before residue depletion.

### Decision 8 — What facts are required for a real case?

**Adopted required fields (9/10):** exact product/NADA, active ingredient and salt, route, dose or feed concentration, frequency, duration, indication, dates/times of first and last administration, flock age/class and health, concurrent drugs, and intended disposition of eggs. Until the veterinarian/FARAD interval is established, eggs should not enter commerce.
