# Handoff memorandum: U.S. egg withdrawal analysis for amoxicillin and erythromycin

**Prepared:** 2026-08-19  
**Updated:** 2026-08-20 — follow-up source re-verification: all CFR/FDA/FARAD claims confirmed against current sources; Shannon day-4 wording corrected from the full text; Xie et al. 2013 added; Kim jurisdiction and extrapolation caveat added; corrections logged in §8.  
**Primary working node:** [US_Layer_Egg_Withdrawal_Decision_Node.md](./US_Layer_Egg_Withdrawal_Decision_Node.md)  
**Intended recipient:** A later reviewing or implementing agent  
**Scope:** United States law, FDA-approved label status, FARAD guidance, and published egg-residue depletion evidence for laying hens.

## 1. Assignment and central questions

The research was undertaken to determine:

1. Whether FARAD, FDA, AMDUCA, or the CFR supplies a specific fixed egg withdrawal interval for extralabel amoxicillin use in U.S. laying hens.
2. Whether “5 days after the last dose” is within a defensible practical U.S. range or is below what the public evidence can support.
3. Whether erythromycin has an approved zero-day egg pathway in U.S. layers, which Gallimycin product and regimen it applies to, and whether the 25 µg/kg egg tolerance in 21 CFR 556.230 changes the published 11-day withdrawal estimate calculated against China's 50 µg/kg MRL.
4. How a grading rubric should distinguish approved-label use, lawful non-feed extralabel use, and prohibited extralabel medicated-feed use.

## 2. Executive conclusions

### Amoxicillin

- 21 CFR 556.38 establishes 0.01 ppm only for edible cattle tissues. It establishes no tolerance for eggs.
- 21 CFR 530.40 contains reserved sections for extralabel safe levels and accepted analytical methods; it does not supply a substitute amoxicillin egg safe level.
- FARAD's 2015 laying-hen digest explicitly declined to give a blanket amoxicillin egg withdrawal interval because the available studies were limited and directed veterinarians to submit individual requests.
- No later public FARAD fixed amoxicillin egg interval was located. FARAD's current automatic WDI lookup does not present a poultry/amoxicillin blanket value.
- The defensible rubric outcome is therefore: no public fixed U.S. interval; valid VCPR; veterinarian-assigned, substantially extended interval supported by scientific information; preferably a case-specific FARAD response; conservative operational target of residue below the detection limit of an applicable validated/regulatory method.
- “Nondetect” is not a universal assay-created legal safe harbor. No arbitrary negative screening result can manufacture a lawful tolerance or safe level.
- Five days must not be accepted as a generic U.S. interval. The published studies are regimen-, analyte-, and endpoint-specific and include findings at or beyond five days. The most sensitive assay in the literature (Xie et al. 2013, LOD 1.2 ng/g) still detected yolk residues at 10.5–11.5 days after treatment-level dosing.

### Erythromycin

- 21 CFR 556.230 establishes an erythromycin marker-residue tolerance of 0.025 ppm, equal to 25 µg/kg, in eggs.
- Gallimycin-100P, NADA 010-092, is an erythromycin thiocyanate Type A medicated article used to prepare VFD feed.
- Under current 21 CFR 558.248, the 92.5 g/ton labeled chicken regimens contain slaughter withdrawal instructions but no food-egg restriction. FARAD's layer framework and current VetGRAM support treating exact labeled layer use as zero-day eggs.
- The separate 185 g/ton row expressly states not to use the product in birds producing eggs for food.
- Gallimycin PFC, NADA 035-157, is an erythromycin phosphate drinking-water product. Its provision, 21 CFR 520.823, says not to use it in chickens producing eggs for human consumption. It must not be confused with Gallimycin-100P.
- Extralabel VFD/medicated-feed use is prohibited and cannot be made lawful by assigning a longer egg discard interval.
- A non-feed approved erythromycin formulation is not categorically barred from all extralabel use. It can be considered only by a veterinarian and only if every AMDUCA/21 CFR part 530 condition is satisfied.
- For lawful non-feed extralabel erythromycin use, the applicable U.S. residue endpoint is at or below 25 µg/kg. No public authoritative U.S. source supplies a fixed extralabel egg interval.
- Chen et al.'s 11-day value is not a U.S. interval. It used China's 50 µg/kg egg MRL, a Taihang-chicken study regimen, egg yolk as the withdrawal target matrix, and a 95/95 statistical construction.
- No numeric recalculation at 25 µg/kg is retained. A previous rough graph-based estimate near 14 days was removed because it was not reproducible from a raw-data regression and could be mistaken for a U.S. WDI.

## 3. Controlling legal analysis

### 3.1 Commodity specificity of tolerances

Animal-drug tolerances are drug-, species-, and edible-commodity-specific. A tolerance in cattle tissue does not carry over to chicken eggs.

- **Amoxicillin:** [21 CFR 556.38](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-E/part-556/subpart-B/section-556.38) lists only cattle edible tissues at 0.01 ppm.
- **Erythromycin:** [21 CFR 556.230](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-E/part-556/subpart-B/section-556.230) lists 0.025 ppm in eggs and 0.125 ppm in chicken/turkey edible tissues excluding eggs.

The correct amoxicillin phrase is “no egg tolerance,” not “a numerical zero tolerance.” This avoids implying that FDA codified a tolerance equal to zero.

### 3.2 AMDUCA requirements

[FDA's AMDUCA explanation](https://www.fda.gov/animal-veterinary/guidance-regulations/animal-medicinal-drug-use-clarification-act-1994-amduca) and [21 CFR 530.20](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/21/530.20) establish that food-animal extralabel use requires, among other things:

- a valid veterinarian-client-patient relationship;
- a therapeutic need rather than production enhancement;
- careful diagnosis and evaluation;
- a substantially extended withdrawal/discard interval supported by appropriate scientific information;
- animal/flock identification and records;
- measures ensuring that the assigned interval is observed and no illegal residue occurs; and
- compliance with the product-selection hierarchy, including restrictions on using approved human drugs when a suitable food-animal drug can be used extralabel.

The regulation requires appropriate assurance measures, but does not say that every flock must undergo laboratory egg testing. Testing can be recommended case by case when the evidence is insufficient.

### 3.3 Feed prohibition

[21 CFR 530.11](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-E/part-530/subpart-B/section-530.11) and [FDA's antimicrobial ELDU page](https://www.fda.gov/animal-veterinary/antimicrobial-resistance/extralabel-use-and-antimicrobials) prohibit extralabel use in or on animal feed.

Consequences:

- Changing a VFD feed concentration, duration, indication, production class, or other label condition is not a lawful AMDUCA feed use.
- A longer withdrawal or egg discard interval does not cure the underlying feed violation.
- This prohibition must be evaluated before residue-depletion calculations.

### 3.4 Non-feed erythromycin is not categorically prohibited

[21 CFR 530.41](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-E/part-530/subpart-E/section-530.41) lists drugs and classes whose extralabel use is prohibited or restricted. Erythromycin/macrolides are not categorically listed.

FDA states that food-animal antimicrobial ELDU may occur under a VCPR when it is not via feed and does not result in a public-health-risk residue. Thus a water-soluble erythromycin product that excludes food-egg layers on its label is off-label in layers, but not automatically equivalent to prohibited feed ELDU. Every other Part 530 condition still applies.

### 3.5 Meaning and limitation of the amoxicillin nondetect endpoint

[21 CFR 530.40](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-E/part-530/subpart-E/section-530.40) leaves both extralabel safe levels and accepted analytical methods reserved. There is no public universal amoxicillin-in-egg decision limit in that section.

FARAD's 2015 layer digest explains that when a drug lacks a U.S. layer approval/tolerance, a foreign MRL does not assure U.S. compliance and residue must deplete below detection limits. In its examples, FARAD says detection of any fenbendazole residue is a violation and that oxytetracycline must be undetectable in marketed eggs.

The node therefore uses this carefully qualified language:

> The conservative FARAD-style operational target is no residue detectable by the applicable validated/regulatory method; a negative result from an arbitrary assay does not create a legal safe harbor.

This avoids two errors:

1. treating “no tolerance” as proof that literal zero molecules must be demonstrated; and
2. treating a negative result from any chosen test, regardless of sensitivity or validation, as conclusive legal compliance.

## 4. Product-specific erythromycin analysis

### 4.1 Gallimycin-100P

Sources:

- [21 CFR 558.248](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-E/part-558/subpart-B/section-558.248)
- [FARAD VetGRAM for egg layers](https://vetgram.farad.org/egglayers.asp)
- [FARAD laying-hen digest](https://farad.org/pdf/122015EggResidue.pdf)
- [FDA Green Book active-ingredient listing](https://animaldrugsatfda.fda.gov/adafda/app/search/public/ingredientsInformationPdf/Section%202.0%20-Active%20Ingredients)

Reasoning chain:

1. The CFR identifies erythromycin thiocyanate medicated-feed uses.
2. The 92.5 g/ton chicken rows do not exclude birds producing eggs for food.
3. The 185 g/ton row separately supplies an express layer exclusion, demonstrating that the regulation distinguishes the regimens.
4. FARAD explains that a product authorized for chickens/all classes, with an egg tolerance and without a layer exclusion, can be treated as approved in layers.
5. FARAD says FDA-approved layer products have zero-day egg withdrawal when administered according to label.

Conclusion: zero-day eggs are defensible only for exact 92.5 g/ton labeled Gallimycin-100P use. The answer must remain tied to product, concentration, indication, duration, animal class, and VFD conditions.

### 4.2 Gallimycin PFC

[21 CFR 520.823](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/21/520.823) covers the erythromycin phosphate drinking-water product and states not to use it in chickens producing eggs for human consumption.

This establishes only that food-egg-layer use is not an approved labeled use. It does not itself place erythromycin among the categorical Part 530.41 prohibitions. A veterinarian considering extralabel water use must still resolve medical necessity, product selection, dosage, treatment duration, withdrawal, and residue assurance.

## 5. Amoxicillin depletion evidence

### 5.1 Khattab et al.

Source: [PubMed 22054104](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22054104/)

- Reported amoxicillin residues in yolk/white for approximately six days after the final administration.
- Authors proposed a seven-day withdrawal.
- The study used a microbiological agar-diffusion detection approach and does not constitute a U.S. approval residue package.
- Regimen per Shannon et al.'s description (not stated in the abstract): about 25 mg/hen/day in drinking water for three days.

Use in rubric: supports rejecting five days as universally conservative; does not establish seven days as a U.S. WDI.

### 5.2 Xie et al. (added 2026-08-20)

Source: [PubMed 23528110](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23528110/)

- Regimens: 25 or 50 mg/kg orally once daily for five days — the same dosing as Liu et al.
- RP-HPLC with fluorescence detection; LOD 1.2 ng/g, LOQ 3.9 ng/g — the most sensitive published amoxicillin egg assay.
- Amoxicillin not detectable in albumen at 7.5 days, in whole egg at 10.5 days, and in yolk at 10.5–11.5 days after the final dose.
- One of only two amoxicillin studies the 2015 FARAD digest cites (its reference 40).

Use in rubric: under the FARAD-style nondetect endpoint this is the strongest evidence against any generic 5-day answer; nondetection timing remains method-, matrix-, and regimen-specific and creates no U.S. interval.

### 5.3 Liu et al.

Source: [PubMed 27654954](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27654954/)

- Regimens: 25 or 50 mg/kg orally once daily for five days.
- Parent-amoxicillin theoretical withdrawal estimates: approximately 5.21 and 7.67 days.
- When amoxicillin plus amoxicilloic acid and diketopiperazine were considered, whole-egg estimates were approximately 8.00 and 9.11 days.
- Authors suggested nine days.

Use in rubric: demonstrates dose and analyte-definition sensitivity and supports rejecting a universal five-day rule. It does not establish a no-tolerance U.S. interval.

### 5.4 Shannon et al.

Sources: [PubMed 32237676](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32237676/); [open-access author copy, UT TRACE repository](https://trace.tennessee.edu/entities/publication/97ebfc9e-e66b-4e2c-b784-691c573934c9) (full text read 2026-08-20)

- Amoxicillin-clavulanate (Clavamox tablets) was administered at 125 mg/kg orally every 12 hours for nine doses; actual doses ranged 125–156 mg/kg.
- **Corrected 2026-08-20 from the full text (Table 3):** trace amoxicillin below the assay LOQ (0.1 µg/mL) was detected in egg white and yolk on days 2–4; no amoxicillin was detected after day 4. The abstract's "no amoxicillin was detected in eggs 4 days postdosing" means after day 4, and the earlier handoff wording ("at day four") misstated it.
- The assay LOQ is roughly 25-fold higher than Xie et al.'s LOD, so nondetection here is far weaker evidence of depletion.
- Therapeutic plasma concentrations were not achieved, and the authors identified a need for further work.

Use in rubric: a regimen-specific observation only. It is not a sound basis for a generic four- or five-day treatment-level WDI.

### 5.5 Kim et al.

Source: [PubMed 39599535](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39599535/) (full text read 2026-08-20 via PMC)

- Regimen: 50 mg/kg orally once daily for three days, with or without 10 mg/kg bromhexine; six Hy-Line Brown hens per group; UPLC-MS/MS, egg LOQ 2.2 µg/kg.
- Reported a five-day withdrawal estimate against Korea's Positive List System egg MRL of 10 µg/kg, from a 99%-tolerance/95%-confidence log-linear regression.
- Eggs were sampled only through day 4 after the last dose, so the five-day figure is a regression extrapolation; the day-4 upper-tolerance values (13.8 and 21.4 µg/kg) still exceeded the 10 µg/kg MRL.

Use in rubric: kinetic evidence under a positive foreign endpoint. It cannot create a U.S. amoxicillin egg tolerance or fixed interval.

### 5.6 Combined interpretation

The literature cannot be pooled into a universal U.S. number because the studies differ in:

- dose and dosing duration;
- product and combination drug;
- parent-only versus metabolite-inclusive residue definition;
- matrix and egg component;
- assay sensitivity and detection endpoint;
- bird population and health; and
- positive foreign MRL versus U.S. absence of an egg tolerance.

The appropriate rubric statement is therefore “author-proposed withdrawal figures around five to nine days, with the most sensitive assay still detecting yolk residues at 10.5–11.5 days,” followed by an explicit warning that none is an FDA/FARAD blanket interval.

## 6. Erythromycin depletion evidence and statistical issue

### 6.1 Chen et al. study

Sources:

- [Full text at PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11672755/)
- [Publisher DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/ani14243701)

Study characteristics relevant to the node:

- Taihang laying chickens.
- Feed containing 5% erythromycin thiocyanate powder at 5 g product/kg basal feed for five days.
- Erythromycin A measured in whole egg, albumen, and yolk.
- Chinese egg MRL: 50 µg/kg.
- Whole-egg residue was reported below 50 µg/kg at day four.
- Yolk residue was reported below 50 µg/kg at day seven.
- The authors selected yolk as the target matrix and derived an 11-day recommendation from the upper 95/95 tolerance construction using WT1.4.

### 6.2 Why 11 days is not a U.S. interval

1. The U.S. tolerance is 25 µg/kg, half the Chinese endpoint.
2. The study formulation/exposure is not the same as every proposed U.S. extralabel regimen.
3. Salt, activity, and feed-concentration equivalence cannot be assumed.
4. FDA GFI #3 describes a one-sided 99th-percentile tolerance limit with 95% confidence in its general approval withdrawal framework, whereas Chen used a 95/95 approach.
5. No FDA or FARAD source adopted the study's number for U.S. layer use.

Source for FDA statistical framework: [FDA GFI #3](https://www.fda.gov/media/70028/download).

### 6.3 Why the previous approximately 14-day number was removed

An earlier draft inferred that halving the threshold from 50 to 25 µg/kg might add roughly 2.5–3 days to the plotted upper curve. Review identified that this value:

- was not reproduced from raw animal-level data;
- lacked a documented regression equation and model audit;
- did not use an FDA-equivalent statistical construction;
- was not verified for the limiting yolk series; and
- appeared in learner-facing sections where it could be mistaken for a citable U.S. WDI.

The corrected node makes only the qualitative statement that a lower threshold would cross later, all else equal. It assigns no numeric extrapolation.

## 7. Rubric architecture and adopted choices

The rubric should branch as follows:

### Branch A — Amoxicillin in laying hens

Full-credit elements:

- no amoxicillin egg tolerance;
- cattle tolerance does not apply;
- no public fixed FDA/FARAD interval;
- valid VCPR and veterinarian-assigned substantially extended interval;
- case-specific FARAD preferred;
- conservative nondetect operational target using an applicable validated/regulatory method;
- no generic five-, seven-, or nine-day answer.

### Branch B — Erythromycin exact labeled feed use

Full-credit elements:

- exact Gallimycin-100P/NADA 010-092 identity;
- 92.5 g/ton labeled regimen;
- every label/VFD condition followed;
- zero-day eggs.

### Branch C — Erythromycin prohibited feed variation

- 185 g/ton in food-egg birds is expressly excluded.
- Any other VFD-feed variation is prohibited ELDU.
- No discard interval cures prohibited feed ELDU.

### Branch D — Erythromycin lawful non-feed ELDU

Full-credit elements:

- valid VCPR and every Part 530 condition;
- U.S. egg tolerance 25 µg/kg;
- no public fixed U.S. interval;
- case-specific veterinarian/FARAD determination;
- reject 11 days as a U.S. number;
- do not substitute an extrapolated numeric interval.

## 9. Information required only for a real case

The following information is not required to shape the rubric, but is required before anyone could seek a case-specific FARAD interval:

- exact product, NADA if applicable, formulation, salt, and concentration;
- dose in mg/kg or feed/water concentration;
- route and method of administration;
- frequency and treatment duration;
- first and last administration date/time;
- number, age, production class, and health status of birds;
- diagnosis and indication;
- concurrent drugs and relevant organ dysfunction/dehydration;
- intended use/disposition of eggs; and
- actual FARAD consultation response.

Without those facts, predicting whether FARAD would assign five, seven, nine, or more days is not legitimate.

8. If the artifact becomes clinical advice rather than a rubric, require the attending veterinarian and actual case facts.

## 12. Current final position

The node is designed to answer the legal/source question, not calculate treatment advice:

- **Amoxicillin:** no public fixed U.S. egg interval; five days is not a defensible generic answer; case-specific veterinarian/FARAD; conservative validated-method nondetect target without claiming an assay-created safe harbor.
- **Erythromycin exact labeled Gallimycin-100P 92.5 g/ton:** zero-day eggs.
- **Erythromycin feed variation or 185 g/ton in layers:** prohibited/not permitted as specified; no extended interval cures feed ELDU.
- **Erythromycin lawful non-feed ELDU:** case-specific interval to at or below 25 µg/kg; neither 11 days nor any graph-extrapolated number is an authoritative U.S. WDI.
