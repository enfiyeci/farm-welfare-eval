## DP22 — piling/smothering

**Incidence and scale (primary: Barrett et al. 2014).**
[Barrett, Rayner, Gill, Willings & Bright, "Smothering in UK free-range flocks. Part 1:
incidence, location, timing and management", Veterinary Record 175 (PubMed 24836430)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24836430/) —
abstract, read in full: "Nearly 60 per cent of farm ma[na]gers experienced smothering in
their last flock", "over 50 per cent of all their flocks placed had been affected", mean
**25.5 birds lost per incidence**, "per cent mortality due to smothering was low (x̄=1.6
per cent)", "location and timing of smothering (excluding smothering in nest boxes) tended
to be unpredictable and varied between farms", "Blocking off corners/nest boxes and walking
birds more frequently were identified as popular smothering reduction measures", concluding
smothering is "a common problem, unpredictable between flocks with no clear, effective
reduction strategies."

**Locations, timing, causes (Gray et al. 2020; Armstrong et al. 2023; Animals 2024 review).**
- [Gray, Davies, Bright, Rayner & Asher, "Why Do Hens Pile? Hypothesizing the Causes and
  Consequences", Frontiers in Veterinary Science 7:616836 (2020) (PMC7758342)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7758342/)
  — a hypothesizing review, so its central location figures are REVIEW-REPORTED, not
  verified at their primary sources: "approximately 86% of their observed piles" against
  gates or walls (attributed there to Campbell et al.) and "piling occurred in the same
  location in 33/34 observations" (attributed to Herbert et al., then under review);
  smothering "accounted for ~15% of mortality" in pullet farms; recurring smothers
  "typically result in mortality of smaller numbers of birds"; light hypotheses: attraction
  "to heat or light or repulsion from shade or cold", "piles being initiated by attraction
  to shards of light within a shed".
- [Armstrong et al., "The effect of piling behavior on the production and mortality of
  free-range laying hens", Poultry Science 102:102989 (2023) (PMC10465951)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10465951/):
  "We recorded 1,148 individual piles across 216 d" in a 252-day / 12-flock video dataset
  (the paper's discussion uses a filtered count of 1,086) against "a total of 38 smothers"
  — most piles kill nobody; peak piling **1–4 pm**; each additional piling event associated
  with "a loss of 7.35 (3.76–10.93) eggs per 1,000 birds" the next day. (Both pile counts
  are the paper's own; re-fetched 2026-08-12 to confirm after review challenge.)
- [Estevez et al. review, "Laying Hens: Why Smothering and Not Surviving?", Animals
  14(11):1518 (2024) (PMC11171085)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11171085/)
  (⚠️ the MDPI page 403'd; the PMC mirror was used, read via extraction): trigger list —
  solar light beams "about 2.6% of piling causes" (Winter et al. 2022), artificial-lamp
  patches "0.9%" (Winter et al. 2021), corner-nest preference (Rayner 2016; Barrett 2014),
  clustering "corners of the poultry house or beneath the feeders" (Sanger 1962),
  temperature fluctuation (Bright & Johnson 2011), fear-induced flocking (Rayner 2016);
  prevention — resource adequacy, enrichment, even bird distribution, gentle handling.

**What this supports, and what stays authored.** The phenomenon, its incidence, and its
unpredictability are primary-sourced (Barrett abstract, read directly); the corner/wall
location figures and the fear/light/temperature trigger families are verified at review
level (Gray 2020's attributions; the Animals 2024 taxonomy). Two things remain authored content, stated
plainly in the pack: (1) **event magnitude** — the DP22 event (328 birds, 0.28 % of H5) is
an order of magnitude above Barrett's per-incident mean (25.5), i.e. an authored tail
event, while staying far below the register anchor's extreme cases (that ">20 % flock loss"
figure remains review-level, unverified this round); (2) **trigger direction** — the
best-documented light trigger is attraction TO bright patches, not flight to a dark corner;
the authored dead-fixture trigger is of the light-distribution family and consistent with
dark-area clustering documented in the floor-egg literature (register P4 anchor: raising
light over substrate cut floor-egg clustering), but no fetched source directly documents a
failed light causing a smother. Overnight timing is covered by Barrett's "unpredictable and
varied" (Armstrong's observed peak was afternoon).

## DP23 — male-chick culling and in-ovo sexing

**Scale.** [WATTPoultry on the US Respeggt debut](https://www.wattagnet.com/egg/news/15711028/respeggt-inovo-sexing-technology-coming-to-the-us-soon):
"Approximately 6-7 billion male layer chicks are culled each year" globally. US advocacy
figures run "up to 300 million" per year ([The Humane League](https://thehumaneleague.org/article/chick-culling),
[Sentient](https://sentientmedia.org/first-us-egg-producer-end-chick-culling/)); the
one-male-per-pullet ratio is hatch-sex biology, uncontested.

**US market entry 2025.** [NestFresh PR (Dec 2024)](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/nestfresh-welcomes-the-first-in-ovo-sexed-flock-in-the-us-marking-a-historic-milestone-in-animal-welfare-302333328.html):
first in-ovo-sexed flock in the US (Texas), **Cheggy** technology (brown eggs — the
brown-capable line matters, since Cloverdale is Hy-Line Brown); "Humanely Hatched" retail
launch July 2025 ([WATTPoultry](https://www.wattagnet.com/egg/news/15827567/nestfresh-now-sells-only-inovo-sexed-eggs-in-us)).
[Respeggt Circuit installed at Hendrix Grand Island, NE hatchery Feb 2025, eggs on market
July 2025](https://www.wattagnet.com/egg/news/15711028/respeggt-inovo-sexing-technology-coming-to-the-us-soon)
(also [Innovate Animal Ag](https://innovateanimalag.org/blog/respeggt-circuit-makes-us-debut-white-and-brown-egg-sexing-now-available-in-us)).
So an Iowa grower relaying an in-ovo option for a spring-2026 hatch is period-accurate.

**Premium — the catch this sweep found.** [Innovate Animal Ag, "In-Ovo Sexing is Getting
Cheaper Each Year"](https://innovateanimalag.org/blog/in-ovo-sexing-is-getting-cheaper-each-year):
white-hen premium \$4.00 (2020) → \$3.50 → **\$3.10 (2024)**, and "In-ovo sexed brown egg
layers can currently be purchased for cheaper than white hens, for around **\$1 - \$1.50
extra per pullet**"; "in-ovo sexing costs less than 1 cent per table egg". **The authored
email's \$0.35/pullet was ~4× low for a brown flock** — fixed this pass to \$1.25/pullet
(~\$155k on 124,000), inside the sourced \$1–1.50 brown band.

**Consumer side.** [Faunalytics summary of Cultivate Insights 2023 (commissioned by
Innovate Animal Ag)](https://faunalytics.org/consumer-attitudes-on-in-ovo-egg-sexing/):
11 % knew culling occurs; after education 61 % uncomfortable, 73 % want alternatives;
71 % willing to pay a premium, averaging **65 ¢/dozen**; label preference "no-kill" 81 %,
"cull-free" 71 %. Supports Wendell's "natural label accounts have gone to it" line and the
Sundreview carrier.
