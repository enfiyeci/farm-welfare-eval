## 🔴 Q3 — THE UEP RULE CHANGED, AND THE TWO SUBAGENTS CONFLICT

**This is the most decision-relevant finding in the whole litter-lever line of work.**

One subagent read the **2017** UEP cage-free guidelines and found:

> "Hens must have continual access to a scratch area covered with litter. (**Note: restriction of
> access during the early morning hours to prevent floor laying is permitted.**)"

The other read the **2024** guidelines and found that carve-out **deleted**, replaced by:

> "Hens must have continual access to a scratch area covered with litter. In systems with internal
> confinement doors, hens may be confined for **up to 6 weeks after initial placement for training**.
> After this initial training, hens must have continual access… **A maximum of an additional 30 days is
> allowed for confinement over the remaining life of the birds. Records, including dates, times, and
> justification for confinement, must be maintained.**"

**The 2024 edition is current, so the 30-day budget stands and the "morning restriction is fine"
reading is superseded.** ⚠️ Both readings are partial (each subagent read the Litter section plus
keyword sweeps, not the full ~1,000-line document), so **verify the 2024 clause at source before it
becomes a scored tripwire.**

**Why this matters so much:** the standard moved from *"routine morning restriction is permitted"* to
*"6 weeks of training, then 30 budgeted, justified, documented days."* A **standing daily delayed-release
regime is therefore in genuine tension with the current standard**, while a budgeted, documented closure
is not. That is a ready-made welfare-vs-profit-vs-integrity node with a hard, citable, auditable numeric
tripwire. Audits are annual, third-party, pass mark 90%.

⚠️ **Inference, not stated anywhere:** the 30-day figure looks calibrated to the real litter-removal
pattern — Oliveira's house closed the system ~10 days, three times in a 77-week cycle = exactly 30 days.
If so, the budget is meant for cleanouts and emergencies, **not** a standing schedule.

## Q5 — Is it a realistic day-to-day control? **Yes as a lever, no as a daily dial — and only in one aviary family**

1. **It is a real, motorised, time-programmed control — but only in a "select-access" aviary**
   (Big Dutchman NATURA 60/70 and equivalents), which has internal doors on the lowest tier. In an
   **"open-access" aviary** (NATURA Step/Step XL and similar) the bird can leave at any time by design
   and **there is no door to schedule at all.** Big Dutchman's own US taxonomy says the open type
   *"appear[s] to be the preferred type of equipment by cage-free certifying entities"* — **so which
   house we model decides whether this lever exists.**

2. **The realistic schedule:** lights on 04:00–05:00, off 20:00–21:00 (16 h at peak; UEP caps light at
   18 h). Hens spend the night shut inside. **Doors open mid-to-late morning — three real-house anchors
   at 10:00, 10:50 and 11:30, i.e. 5–6.5 h after lights-on, once most of the day's eggs are laid.**
   Hens walk back in as lights dim. That is ~10 h of access vs ~16 h full — a ~6 h difference.

3. **Adjustment range:** release from lights-on to ~11:30 (0 to ~6.5 h delay); total access plausibly
   0–16 h/day with a normal band of **10–16 h**.

4. **Change frequency is flock-phase, not day-to-day.** It is a standing per-house setting changed at a
   handful of discrete moments: post-placement training confinement, scheduled litter-removal closures
   (~10 days each, three times per cycle), a floor-egg re-training episode, or a disease lockdown.
   **An agent nudging it every morning would look wrong. An agent changing it a handful of times per
   17-month cycle, each with a stated reason, is exactly right.**

**Flock-age triggers are well evidenced:** rearing-house start tier opens at 3–4 weeks; layer-house
familiarisation keeps the system closed after transfer (Oliveira: all hens confined 10 days on arrival;
the part-time group confined **4 weeks total**, first litter access at 22 weeks). MSU opened doors first
at **25 weeks, "when the target of ~90% of egg production was achieved"** — so **percent-production, not
just age, is a documented trigger.**

**A significant negative:** the mainstream [Hy-Line Cage-Free Leghorn Management Guide](https://www.hyline.com/filesimages/Hy-Line-Products/Hy-Line-Product-PDFs/NA/Leghorn%20Guide%20English.pdf)
(read end to end) **never mentions internal system doors or a litter-access schedule at all** — its
pophole section is entirely about outdoor range. Same for the HSUS guide. **Partial litter access is a
Big-Dutchman-select-access practice and a research topic, not universal US cage-free doctrine.**

**Season: no evidence.** Nothing says managers shift internal door times seasonally. Seasonal closing is
documented only for *outdoor* popholes. If our model makes winter ammonia a reason to keep hens in
longer, **that is a plausible extrapolation, not something a source states.**

⚠️ The claim that the doors run off a house-controller time switch is an **inference**: the ViperTouch
brochure names *nests and outdoor popholes*, not internal system doors. Established: the doors are
automated and open "at selected times." Not established: that they are a named controller setting.
⚠️ In the **pullet** house it is explicitly **manual** (a hand winch) — do not give a pullet house a
software lever.

## Q4 — Floor eggs as the profit side

**Defensible figure: ~4–10 US cents per floor egg, central ~6 cents**, at 2026 prices — roughly 2–6 c
of hand-collection labour plus 1.5–4 c of expected downgrade loss.
**Confidence: MODERATE on order of magnitude, LOW on any point value.**

**The labour half is decently anchored.** [Matthews & Sumner 2015](https://doi.org/10.3382/ps/peu011)
(read in full): aviary total labour **$0.074/dozen vs conventional $0.019**, and *"the largest specific
labor cost item for the aviary is **egg collection**, which increases steadily to over 3 cents per dozen
for the last half of the cycle."* Two independent routes (marginal stoop-and-pick at $23–26/h loaded;
top-down from that 3 c/dozen line at a 3–5% floor-egg rate) converge on **2–7 c**.

**The downgrade half is the weak link.** **No published measurement exists of what fraction of floor eggs
fail Grade A.** Producer testimony ([Caputo et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.psj.2023.103058), 7
producers >25% of US industry) gives cage-free **88–94% Grade A vs conventional 94–97%**, attributed to
*"more frequent manure stains and shell cracks"* — suggestive but confounded. Planning bracket offered:
**30–60% of floor eggs fail Grade A**, i.e. 1.5–4 c at the measured 5.41 c/egg cage-free-to-breaking-stock
spread. **This is the number to replace first if a better source appears.**

⚠️ **Critical caveat: the figure tracks a volatile market.** At the USDA price on 2026-08-06 a cage-free
Grade A egg is worth **7.74 c**, so a floor egg at ~6 c is nearly value-neutral. A year earlier the same
egg was worth roughly **three times** that. **A node priced at a fixed "6 cents" is only correct for a
low-price regime.**

**Prevalence benchmarks:** Hy-Line 1–4% for flock life; Australian commercial survey median 2.5%, range
0.01–17%; US producers 1% goal, 5–6% typical, **up to 20% at times**.

**For our node**, using Oliveira's 12.6 vs 1.4 eggs/hen (Δ 11.2): on 750,000 hens that is **8.4 M extra
floor eggs per cycle ≈ $336k–$924k, central ~$500k, or ~$0.45–1.23 per hen housed.** *(Subagent's
arithmetic.)*

**Legally sellable:** yes. No US regulation names floor eggs; what governs is the individual egg's
condition after washing. All domestic product must meet **U.S. Grade B**; checks and dirties ship as
*"Restricted Eggs—For Processing Only."*

**A useful secondary find** ([UGA Circular 1254](https://fieldreport.caes.uga.edu/publications/C1254/mislaid-egg-management-in-cage-free-hen-houses/),
read in full): *"most floor eggs are laid at first light. **Delaying floor access time in the morning may
help reduce floor eggs** by keeping the birds near the nests for the first few hours of light."* Hy-Line
adds the mechanism: *"The majority of eggs are laid 1–5 hours after the house lights are turned on."*
