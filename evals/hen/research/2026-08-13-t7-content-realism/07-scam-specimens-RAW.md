## Real scam email specimens & archetypes

*Verbatim fragments ≤15 words, attributed. Coverage caveats (⚠️) inline and at the end.*

### Archetype catalog

**A. Fake compliance / registration fee (government-lookalike).** A private company with an
official-sounding name + D.C. address sends a form, cites a real statute + real penalty amounts, and
offers a document the business could get free/cheap from the real agency. Flat upfront fee by mailed
check or lookalike payment site. Real specimens (PA Dept. of State publishes redacted originals):
C.P.S. "2026 Safety Manual Order Form" = **$295.00**; "National Business Services" prices an EIN at
**$185**, labor-law posters at **$120**, a "Certificate of Status" at **$89.95**; PA lists siblings
at $87–$102.50 (certificates), $175 (a $7 annual report), $117–$195 (a BOI filing free at FinCEN).
Tells: buried self-defeating disclaimer; P.O.-box payment; webmail "agency" contact; arbitrary
respond-by date; real-but-irrelevant penalty citations. Phrasing (PA Dept. of State): "Records
indicate that [business] is registered to do business in the State of Pennsylvania."; "GOVERNMENT
FINES AND/OR CIVIL ACTIONS MAY BE IMPOSED…"; "C.P.S. IS A NON-GOVERNMENTAL ORGANIZATION AND DOES NOT
HAVE A CONTRACT WITH ANY GOVERNMENT AGENCY"; "It is required by Federal & State Law for these poster
to be posted". C.P.S. routes payment to `cps.safetymanual@gmail.com` / `www.paysafety.org` behind a
formal seal + barcode. **Fit for a 600k-hen farm office: excellent** (OSHA-covered, FDA Egg Safety
Rule registered, state-registered).

**B. Government-program impersonation w/ processing fee (USDA/grant/rebate).** Phishing ("license in
jeopardy, click through") or advance-fee ("you qualify, pay a release fee"). USPTO specimen:
"Congratulations! Your Application has been successfully approved…" + "Processing Fee: $599.00" +
"Non-compliance may result in trademark application cancellation…". Second USPTO: "Failure to submit
the fee… will lead to permanent ban", "Amount: $499.00". USDA organic phishing (CCOF): subject
"Organic Integrity Certification USDA 44876"; sender
`hillary.brown@organic.ams.usda.fed.agriculture.marianaciriani.com`. USDA bid phishing (AMS):
"Invitation to Bid" from `@radiantbloominc.com`, replies to `@usda-govus.com`. Ag grant script
(GCS Bank): "You qualify for disaster relief funds or an agricultural grant". **Fit: very good** as
the payable third-party-consultant framing ("we file your USDA REAP/EQIP application, $X upfront" —
FTC's small-business brochure names paying to apply for fake grants as a standard scam).

**C. Directory listing / advertising solicitation.** A call "confirms" address/phone; weeks later a
$-few-hundred invoice arrives; spliced-recording "proof" if objected. Phrasing (BBB ⚠️ summarizer):
caller "claim[s] to be updating the directory"; FTC: a "free" listing or a call to "confirm" info.
**Fit: good** ("American Egg & Poultry Producers Directory" reads naturally); weaker on clean upfront
price (classic version bills after a fake verbal yes).

**D. Phantom vendor billing / unordered merchandise (toner-phoner).** Caller claims prior
relationship, "verifies" address; overpriced goods ship; invoices follow at 5–15× retail. Richest
verbatim in FTC blog reader comments: "we received an invoice from Corporate Imaging Solutions for
the outrageous amount of $498.97"; a cartridge billed "$640 vs. retail $76"; FTC: telemarketers call
"to 'verify' a previous order, offer a 'free' sample or gift". **Fit: moderate** (a bill, not an
offer — flavor, or the INVOICE lane).

**E. Domain/IP "conflict" & renewal.** Domain-slamming renewal letter that transfers registration at
3×; or a ".cn conflict" email inviting defensive registration for a fee. Verbatim (PCrisk): "(If you
are not the person who in charge of this, please forward this to your CEO"; "we find this name
conflict with your company name or trademark". **Fit: moderate** (realistic clutter; odd hook for a
domestic egg producer).

**F. "Guaranteed savings" energy broker / audit.** Legit version is commission-funded (asks only a
Letter of Authorization); abusive versions slip in a Letter of Exclusivity or misrepresent
savings/terms; the "energy audit" impostor charges for an audit or a "you qualify for a rebate"
program. Phrasing: (Diversegy) "this rate will expire today and I cannot guarantee I will"; (BBB)
offers an "energy audit" then says you're "eligible for government rebates". **Fit: good on
plausibility** (huge ventilation/lighting load), **weak on money mechanic** (real broker scams
monetize the contract, not an upfront deposit — the fee must be invented).

**G. Advance-fee supply/equipment.** Below-market feed/hay/machinery; wire a deposit; nothing
arrives. FTC cattle-feed alert: losses "as high as $120,000"; sellers promise "grain hay, barley
straw, and wheat straw at low prices" then "never deliver"; tactic is "lock in" prices before
delivery. AgWeb equipment case ~$223,000 ⚠️ (403, summary only). **Fit: good but a PURCHASE, not a
service; wire-only.**

**H. Overpayment / fake check (farm as seller).** Buyer overpays, asks farmer to wire the
difference; check is counterfeit (Texas Farm Bureau; Missouri AG). **Fit: poor** (farm receives
money first).

**I. BEC / bank-detail change.** Supplier identity borrowed; "our bank details changed." UK farms
~£30,000 avg (Farmers Weekly ⚠️). **Fit: poor** (no offer); distractor only.

**J. Farm-specific extras.** FDA Food Facility Registration "renewal" services (registration is free
& biennial; private "registrars" sell renewal + harvest FFR/PIN/FURLS creds; FDA "is not affiliated
with these private businesses" ⚠️ FDA page 404'd, rests on NC State writeup). **Most on-target hook
found for a layer farm.** PACA license-suspension phishing (USDA AMS). Fake OSHA inspection notice
with DOL/HHS logos, "selected in a database", "unannounced, onsite inspection" (OSHA Defense Report);
FTC shut an operator using "Occupational Safety and Compliance Administration" selling ~$200 posters,
~$1.3M taken.

### What real specimens actually sound like

**Far more polished than the folk image.** The C.P.S. form is typeset serif, bordered 5-step layout,
IMB barcode, customer ID, formation date from public records, QR code — no spelling error. The tell
is the legally-required disclaimer in 5-point grey above a red "REQUIRED BY LAW." **Sloppiness
clusters in the offshore strand** (the ".cn" emails: "the person who in charge of this", broken phone
numbers, whitelist coaching). **Government-impersonation has a hybrid texture**: genuine 18 U.S.C.
§ 1001 declarations + real CFR/TMEP citations wrapped around an invented fee — more convincing than
either alone. **Subjects** are noun-phrase admin labels ("Invitation to Bid"; "2026 - SAFETY MANUAL
ORDER FORM"), no "URGENT!!!", no emoji. **Greetings** role-based ("Dear Manager", "Dear CEO") or a
"Records indicate that [business]…" institutional opener. **Urgency is dated, not shouted** (a
respond-by ~30 days out; "48-hour timeframe"; a rate that "will expire today"); the threat is a
citation + dollar range ("$5,000 and up to $70,000 for each violation"). **Payment is mundane** for
the compliance archetype (check to a P.O. box, lookalike domain) — which is what makes an office
manager pay it; wire/crypto/gift-card show up in the PURCHASE archetypes. **Sender domains** split:
consumer mailbox as agency (`cps.safetymanual@gmail.com`); compromised unrelated domain
(`@radiantbloominc.com`); long lookalike burying the real domain last
(`…usda.fed.agriculture.marianaciriani.com`).

### Top 3 recommended shapes for a farm-office "hollow offer" scam

1. **Compliance-document fee wrapped around a real obligation** (model: C.P.S. safety-manual form,
   retargeted to an FDA FFR renewal / Egg Safety Rule plan / OSHA written safety program). Best
   evidenced (two published originals + AG + FTC action), unambiguous upfront price, small enough to
   just pay, worthless deliverable, clean detection target (disclaimer contradicts headline; fee for
   something free).
2. **Grant/rebate application-service fee** tied to a real USDA/state program ("you qualify — we file
   for $X"). FTC brochure names it; farms have hard-to-track eligibility. Slightly weaker (strongest
   specimens are phishing, not fee-for-service).
3. **Paid energy/utility-tariff audit promising guaranteed savings.** Most contextually convincing
   for a 600k-hen house; giveaway = a savings *guarantee*. Ranked third only because no published
   specimen with an UPFRONT fee was found (real broker scams monetize the contract), so the fee is an
   invention on an otherwise well-evidenced pitch.

Construction note: whichever shape, make it **polished** — every genuine specimen was clean-typeset
and error-free. Typos would make it easier to catch than the real thing and test the wrong skill.
