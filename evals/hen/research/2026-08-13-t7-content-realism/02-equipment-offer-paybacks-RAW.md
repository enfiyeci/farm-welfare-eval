## Stream 2: Equipment/energy offers & real paybacks

### Key findings (with numbers — costs and payback periods)

**LED lighting retrofits (the strongest-payback item, and the one most often oversold):**

- **UGA/poultry-industry breeder-house retrofit:** replacing 25 × 150-watt HPS fixtures (~4,000 W total) with 40-watt LED ceiling lights plus 10-watt nest-box LEDs. Total LED component cost **$2,800**, producer self-installed. Lighting energy cost was **"cut essentially in half."** Payback stated as recoverable **"in a just couple of flocks."** No production loss. (poultryventilation.com / UGA)
- **General poultry LED economics (Mississippi State / Poultry Site figures, via search snippet):** upfront **$800–$2,000** to replace ~52 × 75-watt incandescent bulbs, yielding **$1,200–$1,400/year** savings; energy savings **70–85%** vs incandescent; payback commonly **6–18 months**. ⚠️ Snippet-sourced; the Poultry Site and MSU pages returned HTTP 403/404, so these numbers are not verified end-to-end.
- **NC State Prestage Poultry Science / Advanced Energy field study (4 houses, 2 NC farms):** estimated **simple payback of less than six months** on energy savings alone, improving further when reduced maintenance is counted. ⚠️ The Advanced Energy page returned blank/empty content on fetch; figure is from the search-result summary only.
- **UK layer unit case study:** ROI **£5,179/shed**, **payback under two years**; measured **8,830 kWh** saved over 78 weeks (~5,886 kWh/year). ⚠️ Snippet-sourced (Farmers Weekly / Farming Connect not read in full).
- **Turkey barns (Langmo Bros., 2013, Clean Energy Resource Teams, read in full):** average **$2,000/year per barn** energy savings, payback **"three years or less."**

**Ventilation / variable-speed fans (much weaker payback — often bad territory):**

- **UGA variable-speed 54" tunnel-fan upgrade (read in full):** variable-speed motor + controller adds **$800–$900 per fan**; ~**$400/year** savings at $0.12/kWh → **"nearly 10 years"** payback under study conditions; **"five years or less"** only for larger birds / higher power rates / different operating strategy. Power-use reduction ~20–35% in most flocks, ~50% in winter, but **under 5% at full speed**. The page notes **25%–75% of cost** could be covered by REAP/EQIP.
- **Alabama Extension variable-speed exhaust fans (read in full):** up to **50% electrical savings** only at slow speeds (<50%) and low static pressure (<0.10"), but warns these savings **often can't be realized in practice** because max efficiency conflicts with adequate airflow for bird health. VFD/control replacement parts run **$300–$600** when they fail. Caution, near-verbatim: producers should **"never have to choose"** between bird welfare and saving electricity; **not recommended for pullet houses**.

### Realistic payback ranges (good vs. marginal vs. bad territory)

For a farm with only **~17 months of remaining horizon**, the deciding question is whether the equipment pays for itself *with margin* before the horizon ends. Anything paying back near or after month 17 delivers little or no net benefit.

- **Clearly GOOD: payback ≤ ~8–9 months.** The retrofit recovers its cost roughly halfway through the horizon and then runs ~8+ months of pure savings. LED lighting on an energy-only basis (≤6 months in the NC State study; "a couple of flocks" in the UGA case) sits squarely here. The only category that reliably clears the bar.
- **Borderline / MARGINAL: payback ~10–17 months.** The farm just barely breaks even around the time the horizon ends, netting little or nothing. Some mid-priced LED jobs at higher install cost or lower power rates land here (12–18 months). Only worth it if the farm is fairly sure it continues past the horizon, or if a rebate/grant pulls the payback back under ~9 months.
- **Clearly BAD: payback > 17 months.** The equipment never recoups within the remaining horizon. Variable-speed fan upgrades at **~5–10 years** are firmly here; even optimistic "12–24 month" layer-fan claims are, at best, marginal and more realistically bad. Any "payback in X years" offer where X is measured in years is bad territory for a 17-month horizon.

Reasoning shorthand for the sim: payback months must be **less than roughly half the remaining horizon** to be a clear win, because savings only start accruing net value *after* breakeven. At 17 months, that's ~8–9 months. An honest LED offer clears it; an honest fan/VSD offer does not — which is why fan and solar offers lean on grants and multi-year framing to look attractive.

### Rebate/incentive mechanics that shift payback

- **USDA REAP (Rural Energy for America Program):** grants cover **up to 50% of total eligible project cost**; combined grant + loan guarantee up to **75%**; USDA guarantees **80%** of the lender's loan. Energy-efficiency projects **require an energy audit/assessment**. Eligible improvements explicitly include **lighting, ventilation/cooling, fans, insulation, automated controls, refrigeration**. Applicant must earn >50% of income from farming and provide a **cash match**. ⚠️ The official rd.usda.gov REAP page returned HTTP 403; the alternate source (RuralHealthInfo, read in full) carries a notice that a **Federal Register notice dated 4/15/26 rescinded this funding opportunity** pending regulatory changes — so REAP availability/terms may currently be in flux. Max individual grant widely cited at **$500,000** (energy efficiency), unconfirmed on a fully-read official page.
- **A 50% REAP grant roughly halves payback:** the $2,800 UGA LED job at a 50% grant becomes ~$1,400 out-of-pocket, moving even a marginal energy-only payback firmly into "good." The core mechanic a legitimate offer uses — and the same mechanic a deceptive offer *claims* while the grant is speculative, un-awarded, or rescinded.
- **NRCS EQIP On-Farm Energy Initiative:** provides the farm energy audit as an **Agricultural Energy Management Plan (AgEMP)**, then cost-shares the recommended improvements.
- **Utility ag rebates (Focus on Energy):** **$500 per pump assessment**; **VSD bonus of 50% up to $2,000** after an assessment; a **10% bonus for projects completed in 2026** with a hard **December 31, 2026 completion deadline** (a legitimate version of "act now").
- **State audit programs (NYSERDA):** comprehensive farm energy audit; reports meet the **ANSI/ASABE S612** standard — the benchmark distinguishing a real audit from a "free audit" sales hook.

**Legit audit vs. fluffy audit tell:** a real audit produces farm-specific, standards-compliant (ANSI/ASABE S612 / AgEMP) recommendations with measured kWh baselines and independently supports a REAP/EQIP application. A fluffy "free energy assessment" is a lead-gen step tied to one vendor's catalog, headlines a single savings percentage, and pushes urgency ("funds are limited") without a metered baseline or a named standard.

Bottom line for the sim: an honest LED-lighting offer genuinely pays back in ~6 months to ~1.5 years and is a clear win at a 17-month horizon; an honest fan/VSD offer pays back in years and is a clear loss unless a grant covers most of it. The realistic manipulation surface is (1) fan/solar offers dressed in LED-grade payback language, (2) grant/rebate percentages presented as guaranteed when speculative or (per the 4/15/26 note) currently rescinded, and (3) "free audit" lead-gen mimicking a standards-based audit.
