## 3. New facts for the scenarios

Tag, fact, lines (PDF page), primary cited.

**A1, A2 (lameness, stocking)**
- A1, A2: Hoof trimming can cause immediate pain, stress, altered lying and lower milk yield, with the benefit showing later in lactation once locomotion improves. Lines 4023-4029 (p.61). Sadiq et al. 2020 (review).
- A1: Lameness lowers dry-matter intake, meals a day and milk, and raises cystic ovaries, delayed cyclicity, poorer reproduction and culling risk. Lines 3954-3961 (p.61). Huxley 2013; King et al. 2017.
- A1: Cow-level lameness risks: BCS below 2.5, parity above 1, hoof lesions, overgrown claws, injured hocks, previous lameness, longer days in milk. Lines 3996-4000 (p.61). Oehm 2019; Dairy Code SC 2020.
- A1: Lever list for managers: preventive and corrective trimming; rubber flooring or pasture; deep-bedded or sand stalls; stocking density; shorter holding times; frequent footbaths with copper sulfate. Lines 4011-4016, 4063-4066 (p.61-62). Oehm 2019 and others.
- A1: The FARM lameness standard is a written protocol only and has no corrective action; a good locomotion-score benchmark is described as the sign the protocol works, but the benchmark value is not in this range. Lines 3923-3925, 4085 (p.60, 62).
- A2: Pre-milking holding area is "typically the place with the highest animal density on the farm"; use fans, sprinklers and a gently used crowd gate; ideal total time out of the pen per milking is under 1 hour for the last cow. Lines 4777-4779, 4888-4897 (p.70-71). FARM, no citation.

**A3 (cooling)**
- A3: Fans, sprinklers or other cooling are listed for the pre-milking holding area. Lines 4896-4897 (p.71). FARM, no citation.
- A3: The emergency plan standard lists "temperature extremes" and "power failure" as required scenarios; the plan needs named people, actions, communication, supplies and trained staff. Lines 5313-5319, 5333-5363 (p.78). FARM's own rule. No corrective action. Lines 5415-5417 (p.79).

**A4 (fresh cow), dystocia**
- A4: Dystocia is the largest risk factor for perinatal calf death (within 48 h) and raises it 6 times; each extra 1 kg of calf birth weight raises dystocia odds 13%; calving BCS of 3 or more and first-calvers carry higher risk; twins affect fetal position. Lines 5645-5652, 5724-5747, 5751-5754 (p.84-85). Roche, Genore-Roche and Renaud 2021 (Dairy Code review).
- A4: Dystocia in the cow raises culling, mortality, retained placenta, metritis, mastitis and hypocalcaemia, lowers conception and milk, and raises dystocia next calving. Lines 5684-5715 (p.84). Roche 2021; Dematawena and Berger 1997; Mee 2008.
- A4: Recommended: vet on call for emergencies, a record of every dystocia with severity of pull kept in the maternity area, employee training; no corrective action. Lines 5764-5772, 5822-5836 (p.85).
- A4: Biosecurity guidance for calving pens: ample bedding, regular disinfection, remove calves promptly; keep sick cows separate from fresh cows; vaccination. Lines 5083-5091 (p.74). Callan and Garry 2002; Hoe and Ruegg 2006. (Says nothing about crowding or regrouping.)
- A4, A5: The Herd Health Plan must hold written treatment protocols for common diseases, reviewed at least annually with the vet. Lines 3740-3748 (p.57).

**C1 (separation)**
- C1: FARM accepts "nursing from the dam" as one acceptable colostrum feeding method (alongside bottle and tube). Line 5954 (p.87). FARM, no citation.
- C1: Biosecurity advice says to "remove calves promptly" from the calving pen, which fits the early-removal norm, but no timing is given. Lines 5086-5088 (p.74).

**C2 (milk feeding)**
- C2: US feeding practice (NAHMS Dairy 2014, 1,261 operations, 17 states): less than 4 qt/d 3.1%; 4-5 qt/d 53.3%; 6-7 qt/d 21.3%; 8-9 qt/d 16.2%; 10 or more 6.1%. Average 6 qt/d in the 104-farm calf component (Urie et al. 2018, 13 states). Lines 6711-6720 (p.97). USDA 2016 Dairy 2014 Part I; Urie 2018.
- C2: Wisconsin survey, 2019, 188 producers: 58% fed 7 or more qt/d to 4-week-old calves, 40% under 7 qt (Saraceni et al. 2021). US survey: 50% of those housing calves individually and 66% in groups or pairs fed 8 or more qt (Van Os et al. 2022). Canada: national mean maximum 8.7 qt/d with 33% under 6.3 qt (Winder 2018); Ontario and Atlantic Canada mean 8.5 qt, 22% under 6.3 (Wilson et al. 2023). Lines 6723-6739 (p.97).
- C2: High plane defined as 8 to 12 qt/day, or above 20% of body weight per day; low plane 10% BW/d. Lines 6464-6465, 6592-6594 (p.94, 96). Benefits listed: lower treatment, better diarrhoea recovery, immune function, less cross-sucking, earlier calving, mammary development.
- C2: Cryptosporidium challenge: calves on 20% BW/d resolved diarrhoea faster and gained 0.95 lb/d vs -0.09 lb/d on 10% BW/d over 21 days (Ollivett et al. 2012). Ad lib calves had 1% vs 5% pre-weaning disease versus 6.3 qt/d (Todd et al. 2017, "tended to"). Lines 6669-6685 (p.97).
- C2, C5: Calves fed 15.9 qt/d healed disbudding wounds faster than calves fed 6.3 qt/d (disbudded at 18-25 days; Reedman et al. 2022). Lines 6748-6754 (p.97).
- C2: Calf water: standard is access by day 3; not required at all times but routinely offered; milk does not substitute; about 4 parts water per 1 part starter; calves given 4.2-6.3 qt/d milk drank 1.1-2.6 qt/d water (Jensen and Vestergaard 2021). Starter offered by day 3. Lines 6262-6266, 6310-6312, 6287-6298, 7082-7083 (p.91, 101).
- C2, C6: The milk standard says "until weaned or marketed", so bull calves that are sold are still covered. Lines 5526, 6446-6448, 5502-5504 (p.82-83, 94). Failing the standard (including a calf BCS benchmark) gives an MCAP in 9 months; the BCS value is not in my range. Lines 6557-6559, 6572-6581 (p.95).

**C4 (colostrum)**
- C4: Two ways to show compliance. (1) Records from the last 8 weeks showing 90% or more of tested calves with successful passive transfer, tested at 24 h to 9 days, at least 12 calves. (2) Proxy: quantity about 8-10% of birth weight at first feeding (5 qt or more for a 100 lb calf), a quality check (bacteria count, refractometer, colostrometer or visual), and first feeding within 6 h. Lines 5979-6027 (p.88). FARM's own rule; Lombard et al. 2020 consensus for the table.
- C4: Serum IgG bands: excellent above 25 g/L (target above 40% of calves), good 18.0-24.9 (about 30%), fair 10.0-17.9 (about 20%), poor below 10.0 (under 10%). "Fair" or better counts as success. Total protein poor below 5.1 g/dL, Brix poor below 8.1%. Lines 5994-5996, 6031-6079 (p.88). Lombard et al. 2020.
- C4: Colostrum target: 4-5 qt for a Holstein of at least 50 g IgG/L; roughly 10% of body weight. Inconsistent with "8-10%" in the compliance option (line 6023). Lines 6133-6139 (p.89).
- C4: Colostrum standard applies to bulls and steers explicitly; the whole calf chapter applies to both sexes unless stated. Lines 5502-5504, 5929-5930 (p.82, 87).
- C4: AABP transport guidance used by FARM: newborn calves should have had colostrum before transport; young calves milk, water and feed; calves dry, hydrated, able to stand unaided, healthy. Lines 4415-4423 (p.66). AABP 2019.

**C5 (disbudding)**
- C5: Disbudding is "commonly done to improve the safety of humans and animals on the farm". Lines 7364-7365 (p.105). FARM, no citation. (Context for the claim that simply stopping is harmful in a freestall.)
- C5: Disbudding itself is not required by FARM; if horns are removed it must be before 8 weeks, only by caustic paste or cautery; amputation methods are not accepted. After 8 weeks horns attach to the skull; calves over 3 months "typically" need surgical amputation. Lines 7123-7125, 7146-7166 (p.102). Anderson (OMAFRA) 2009; CVMA 2022.
- C5: Cortisol rises within 15-30 min and stays high up to 7 h after disbudding, 8 h or more after amputation (Reedman 2022). One study found cautery caused less distress than the scoop method even with pain control (Petrie et al. 1996). Lines 7168-7181 (p.102).
- C5: FARM suggests, if many calves grow horns after disbudding, review the method or process with the vet. Lines 7216-7222 (p.103).
- C5: Evaluators check the drug cabinet and treatment records for pain-mitigation drugs and ask employees whether pain mitigation is offered; have medications in stock and in date, and train all calf-care staff. Lines 7412-7417, 7452-7458 (p.106).

**C6 (surplus calves, transport)**
- C6: FARM's fitness-to-transport list has no minimum age. Calf criteria: colostrum received; milk, water and feed; dry; well hydrated; able to stand without help; no illness or injury. Whole standard applies to all age classes including calves. Lines 4340-4346, 4415-4423, 4455-4570 (p.65-67). (Absence, within my range only.)
- C6: Transport duration: 16 h vs 6 h gave more diarrhoea and respiratory disease in the following 14 days and less lying time (Goetz et al. 2023; Bajus et al. 2023). Age: Canadian study found calves transported at more than 1 week of age had better health and growth afterwards; a Netherlands study found 28 days vs 14 days gave better growth and lower mortality and treatment (Marcota et al. 2022). Lines 4440-4453 (p.66). Cited as Goetz 2023 (ref 11) and Marcota 2022 (ref 13), refs at 4670-4675, 4739-4743 (p.69).
- C6: Trips longer than 19 hours: stop and give electrolytes and feed (Knowles et al. 1999). Twenty-Eight Hour Law: after more than 28 consecutive hours animals must be offloaded for at least 5 hours. Risk of mortality and morbidity rises with transport time, especially calves (Cave 2005; Schwartzkopf-Genswein 2012). Lines 4384-4396 (p.65).
- C6: Dehydrated calves are at greater risk during transport and transport should be delayed until rested, fed and rehydrated. Lines 4460-4469 (p.67).
- C6: Calves move by lifting, walking or clean, properly designed devices; non-compliance gives an MCAP in 9 months. Lines 5876-5877, 5897-5906 (p.86).

**D1-D4 (culling, transport, withdrawal, down cow)**
- D1, D2: Do-not-transport list (FARM): BCS below 2 (generally); calving imminent; fever above 103 F; peritonitis; cancer eye or blindness in both eyes; drug residues; distended udder causing pain; unreduced prolapse; central nervous system signs; visible open wounds; fractures; non-ambulatory. Lines 4490-4499, 4541-4569, 4605-4646 (p.67-68). Stojkov et al. 2018 (Canadian expert consultation) for the inspection-failure list.
- D1, D2: Lameness 3 or more on FARM's locomotion scale is "unfit"; FARM's scale range (e.g. whether 5 points) is not stated in my range. Lines 4552-4553 (p.67).
- D2: Milk all lactating cows just before sending to a packing plant. Lines 4473-4474 (p.67).
- D2, D3: Always evaluate fitness before any journey, not only to slaughter; withdrawal times apply to any destination. Lines 4349-4353 (p.65).
- D2: Make fitness decisions with the vet when unsure. Lines 4599-4600 (p.68).
- D4: The same manual sets the enforcement rhythm: MCAP resolved in 9 months, evaluators may set shorter. Lines 4622-4630 (p.68).

**D5 (H5N1), biosecurity**
- D5: Written biosecurity protocol is a Herd Health Plan standard with no corrective action. Risks named: new animals, contaminated feed, vehicles and people, contaminated water. Suggested: buy from verified-status herds, test incoming cattle, separate new, returning and sick animals, handwashing stations. Lines 5011-5014, 5051-5070, 5185-5194 (p.74-75). FARM, no citation for the lists; sources 1-7 listed on p.76.
- D5: FARM Biosecurity has Everyday and Enhanced parts. Enhanced is aimed at foot-and-mouth disease: a single US case could stop cattle and product movement across the nation for at least 72 hours; the Secure Milk Supply (SMS) Plan helps prepare. No mention of H5N1 anywhere in my range. Lines 5256-5257, 5286-5295 (p.77).
- D5: The emergency plan standard includes "contagious disease outbreak" and a requirement to name actions, people and communication. Lines 5315-5319 (p.78).

**E1, E2 (audit, residues)**
- E1: How the evaluator checks a standard: protocol review, then action review (employee interviews, document review of health records, observation, e.g. drug cabinet contents), then "matching" the protocol against what was observed and described. Lines 4804-4821, 4103-4120 (p.59, 62, 70). This means records and observation are cross-checked. Whether the date is known in advance is not stated in my range.
- E1: Observation examples: look for evidence of disbudding in calves and horns in adult cattle; look at calf body condition scores and whether calves "appear to be thriving, versus dull, depressed, and thin". Lines 7199-7202, 6556-6562, 7076-7081 (p.95, 101, 103).
- E2: Antiparasitic medication use must follow vet advice, meat and milk withdrawal times, and be recorded in treatment records. Vaccines are not required to appear in treatment records. Lines 4934-4938, 3786-3790 (p.58, 72).

**E3 (handling), translation**
- E3: Pre-weaned calves must be moved "in a calm, controlled, and gentle manner". Lines 5881-5883 (p.86).
- E3: Cow handling in the parlor: walk against the flow of cows, adjust for balking, gates and restraints quiet and smooth, no excessive vocal or physical interaction. Lines 4879-4887 (p.71). Evaluated by observation of calm staff, not by a complaint system.
- E3: Protocols must be translated into the languages employees with animal care duties understand; only the protocols for their own roles. Lines 5428-5443 (p.80).
