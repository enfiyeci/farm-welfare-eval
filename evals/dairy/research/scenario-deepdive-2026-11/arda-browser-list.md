# Sources only Arda can open at a browser (all eight scenarios)

Written 2026-11-11. Each source below was blocked to scripts (paywall, bot check, or HTTP 403 with no archived copy) during the source checks of the eight deep-research answers. Each entry says what reading it would settle. The same lists sit at the end of each scenario's `verified.md`.

## A1 (`A1-lame-cows/`), 8 sources

1. Beggs et al. 2019, J Dairy Sci 102:1522, https://doi.org/10.3168/jds.2018-14847: whether 62% (August full-text read) or 60% (abstract) of lame cows were in the last 30% of the milking order, and which scores form the base of the 24% farmers found.
2. Groenevelt et al. 2014, Vet J 201:283, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tvjl.2014.05.017: cows per arm, how long usual-care cows waited, how many farmers treated, and whether treatment included a block or pain relief (decides how far A1.6 can rise above W).
3. Leach et al. 2012, Vet J 193:626, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tvjl.2012.06.043: the lameness prevalence difference 4 weeks after treatment, re-treatment counts and time to treatment per arm (A1.6).
4. Whay et al. 1998, Vet J 156:23, https://doi.org/10.1016/S1090-0233(98)80058-0: per-lesion numbers and threshold sizes at day 28, and whether treated cows received an NSAID (A1.5).
5. Laven et al. 2008, N Z Vet J 56:210, https://doi.org/10.1080/00480169.2008.36835: pain thresholds at days 28 and 100 against sound cows (pain duration).
6. Tadich et al. 2013, Vet J 197:401, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tvjl.2013.01.029: whether cows at score 2 of 5 had a lower pain threshold than sound cows (moderate lameness).
7. Cook et al. 2016, J Dairy Sci 99:5879, https://www.journalofdairyscience.org/article/S0022-0302(16)30215-6/fulltext: the lameness range across the 66 Wisconsin herds and the sand against mattress effect size (A1.1, A1.7).
8. "Lameness in Cattle", https://www.researchgate.net/publication/380892767_Lameness_in_Cattle (probably the 2024 Springer chapter, doi 10.1007/978-3-031-51788-4_17): what it says about early treatment and recurrence.

## D4 (`D4-down-cow/`), 8 sources

2. Poulton et al. 2016b, Aust Vet J 94:173, https://doi.org/10.1111/avj.12445 (Wiley 403): whether the journal tables match the thesis (eventual recovery 45%, 41%, 8%, 0% by care tier).

4. Stojkov et al. 2016, J Dairy Sci 99:2076, https://doi.org/10.3168/jds.2015-10448 (403): group sizes and P value behind "65% recovered with good nursing, 90% failed with poor".
5. AABP Guidelines for the Humane Euthanasia of Cattle 2023, https://aabp.org/Resources/AABP_Guidelines/EUTHANASIA-2023.pdf (error page to curl): any stated basis for the four-hour and 24-hour limits.
6. California Penal Code 599f, https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PEN&sectionNum=599f (bot check): confirms the mirror text used for the ban on shipping a down cow.
7. Shpigel et al. 2003, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12846288/ (full text not open): the day 1 to day 4 recovery decline (76% to 19%), now known only second-hand.
8. McFarlane et al. 2022, J Dairy Sci 105:2544, https://doi.org/10.3168/jds.2021-21046 (403): any newer synthesis on nursing quality and time down.
