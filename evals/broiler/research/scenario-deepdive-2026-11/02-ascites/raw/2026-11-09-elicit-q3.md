Yes—most of the experimental work in our corpus indicates that cold exposure / low brooding temperature in early life can increase ascites risk in broilers, especially via effects on pulmonary hypertension and right-heart dysfunction.

Increased ascites with cold during early weeks: İpek & Sahan (2006) found higher ascites-related mortality with a cold-stress temperature program in weeks 1–3 (9.52% vs 1.90% at week 3 and week 6) and greater right-ventricle changes (higher RV/TV ratio).
Mechanistic cold studies: Julian et al. (1989) reported higher mortality from right ventricular failure/ascites under 13°C vs 23°C; cold increased oxygen demand and worsened pulmonary vascular capacity mismatch. Wang (2002) similarly found higher pulmonary arterial pressure, RV/TV and ascites incidence in cold-treated birds.
Direct “cold-induced ascites” outcomes: Abdulkarimi et al. (2017) and Daneshyar et al. (2009) both report that cold-reared birds had greater right-ventricle enlargement/indices and higher ascites mortality.

Important nuance: not all cold exposure is harmful. Bahadoran et al. (2010) reported that early cold exposure (3 h at 15°C at day 3–4, with additional low temperature later) was associated with lower ascites mortality—interpreted as an adaptation/thermotolerance effect.

If you tell me whether you mean true acute chilling vs milder reduced brooding temperature, and the age window (e.g., day 1–7 vs day 1–14), I can help narrow which evidence best matches your scenario.
