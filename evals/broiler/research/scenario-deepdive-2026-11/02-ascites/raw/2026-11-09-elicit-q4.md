Which feeding, lighting, ventilation or dietary supplement interventions reduce ascites in broiler chickens?
6:27 PM

Interventions with evidence (from the papers in the table) that reduce ascites in broiler chickens include:

Lighting / photoperiod (reduced metabolic/oxygen demand)

Intermittent lighting (e.g., 1L:3D vs 23L:1D) reduced ascites mortality/incidence in multiple studies (Buys et al., 1998; Hassanzadeh et al., 2000; Hassanzadeh et al., 2003; Wang, 2007; dark-length schedule study, 2012).
Light restriction/shorter photoperiod in early life reduced ascites deaths (Lott et al., 1996) and increasing dark exposure reduced ascites (Iranian study, 2012).

Feeding / diet composition (limit growth/metabolic load; lower protein)

Low-protein diets helped prevent ascites under cold-stress conditions (Canan Bolukbasi & Emsen, 2006).
Feed restriction reduced ascites incidence (McGovern et al., 1999), though it also reduced growth.

Dietary supplements / nutrients (antioxidant or cardio-respiratory support)

Coenzyme Q10 reduced ascites mortality in broilers challenged with low ambient temperature (Geng et al., 2004).
Vitamin C and vitamin E (with/without Fang Fusan) reduced ascites incidence vs control (Wang, 1999).
Reviews summarizing additional supplement strategies (e.g., higher vitamins C/E, selenium yeast; n-3 oils) are also included (Aftab & Khan, 2005; Singh et al., 2011).

Ventilation / environmental management (oxygenation and avoiding cold stress)

While the stronger “trial-style” evidence in this set is for lighting/diet/supplements, reviews and management papers emphasize adequate ventilation/air quality and avoiding cold stress to reduce ascites risk (Ezzulddin, 2023; Effective Management Practices, 2009; Singh et al., 2011).
