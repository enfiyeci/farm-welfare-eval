What is the incidence of sudden death syndrome (flip-over) in commercial broiler chickens by age, sex and strain?
6:19 PM

From the papers retrieved so far, I can’t give a complete, quantitative incidence table of “sudden death syndrome (flip-over) by age × sex × strain,” because most abstracts report only (i) broad age windows (e.g., peak at ~3–4 weeks), (ii) sex differences (males/cockerels higher), or (iii) overall incidence ranges—without breaking incidence down simultaneously by age and strain.

What the retrieved evidence does indicate:

Age pattern (all/overall flock): SDS affects broilers from ~2 days to market age, with peak mortality in weeks 3–4 (Meshram & Bijoy 2017; Siddiqui et al. 2009; Ononiwu et al. 1979).
Sex pattern: Cockerels/males higher incidence than pullets/females (Ononiwu et al. 1979; Meshram & Bijoy 2017; Siddiqui et al. 2009). In a male-only ECG study of 300 birds, 8 birds died from SDS (Olkowski et al. 2004), but this does not provide age- and strain-specific female/male rates.
Strain: At least one commercial strain-cross study reports mortality differences generally, but does not provide SDS-specific incidence in the abstract (Bilgili et al. 2006).
Quantitative incidence range (overall): A field-based/induced-work summary reports average SDS mortality ~0.61% to 2.49% across 18 farms, but not stratified by age×sex×strain (Praveen et al. 2020).

If you want, I can run a follow-up targeted search for papers that explicitly report SDS incidence stratified by age and sex and ideally include specific strain/cross (e.g., Cobb vs Ross; strain-cross pen data). Tell me whether you need percent of birds vs mortality rate per week/1000, and what age resolution you prefer (e.g., day 1–7, 8–21, 22–42, 43–market).
