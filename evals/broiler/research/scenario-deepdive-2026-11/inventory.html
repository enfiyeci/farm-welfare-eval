Which broiler welfare claims have a source

Every welfare-effect claim in the 20 candidate broiler scenarios, taken from the six research memos of September 21 and checked against two documents found in the November Apify pull. Scenarios are ranked by claims with no source or a conflicting source, then by claims resting on weak sources.

24 no source12 conflicts35 weak52 secondary13 verified

What the labels mean

  • No sourceNo source. The memo states it but names no source, or only says 'task anchor', 'snippet' or 'reviews'.
  • ConflictsConflicts. A source we have read points the other way, or the cited study does.
  • WeakWeak source. A source is named, but only its abstract, a search summary or a page extract was read.
  • SecondarySecondary. A review or standard we read in full states it and names the primary study; the primary is not read.
  • VerifiedVerified. The primary source was read in full.

What counts as one claim is my judgment, so read the counts as an order, not a measurement. Standards thresholds, costs and food-safety claims are left out; only claims about what happens to the birds are counted.

Core prompt (Gemini, ChatGPT, Perplexity)

I am building a research-based simulation of a commercial US broiler (meat chicken) farm, used to test whether an AI farm manager looks after animal welfare. I need primary, checkable evidence on leg disorders in fast-growing broilers (Ross 308, Ross 708, Cobb 500 and similar), compared with slower-growing breeds where possible.

PART 1. Check these four claims. For each one, say whether the evidence supports it, partly supports it, contradicts it, or whether you found nothing. Give the actual figure the source reports, with the breed, bird age, country, year, sample size and how the condition was diagnosed (gait score, live visual exam, or necropsy).
