Water, mortality, house

  • Water: Ross puts healthy intake at 1.6-1.8 litres per kg of feed at 21 °C (025). Neither breed publishes a water-by-age curve. Hubbard prints feed × 1.70 as a daily column without a unit (080). checked
  • Mortality: Cobb's only broiler target is cumulative 7-day loss at or under 1% (051). Ross gives none. The US grower survey average is 3.74% per flock (161, 2011). checked
  • Cobb caps density at 42 kg/m² in tunnel houses with pads, 30 in open-sided houses (051). Ross suggests keeping houses under 21 °C from day 21 (024). checked

Lighting and catch

  • Ross: 4-6 hours of dark after day 7, at under 0.4 lux; 30-40 lux early, then at least 5-10 (025). checked
  • Cobb: four lighting programmes by target weight, darkness starting once chicks reach 130-180 g (051). agent
  • Both breed guides: take feed away 8-12 hours before processing, water until catching (025, 051). checked

The default call-out, Agri-Alert 800 family (083, read in full)

5 min
Siren

Starts when the alarm is validated and sounds for 5 minutes while the call-out runs.

1 min

Counted from validation, alongside the siren; lets someone on site acknowledge first.

1 min
Between calls

Up to 8 numbers in order, message played 3 times per call.

7×
Passes

The list repeats up to 7 times. The 800EZe then acknowledges the alarm itself (084).

30 min
Recall

After acknowledgement, if the zone is still bad, the whole call-out starts again.

A caller acknowledges with a 4-digit code. The 800/800T manual says an alarm stays active until a person acknowledges it (083); the self-acknowledgement at the end of the call-out is stated in the 800EZe manual (084). Loss of the dialer's own 16 VAC supply alarms after 5 minutes. On a long outage a flat battery puts the 800 and 9600 into standby, and from then on nothing is watched. The 9600 adds four phone groups with separate day, night and weekend lists, with configurable switch times (shown as 08:00 and 17:00 in the manual). The newer 128 Touch manual lists the same settings without defaults and warns that its e-mail and text alerts can arrive hours late (085, ⚠️ partial read). agent The call start delay, time between calls and 7 passes are checked.

How fast losses come

  • After exhaust fans stop, birds begin to die about 20 minutes after the house reaches 85-90 °F near saturation (g100, UGA 1994). checked
  • Modern houses can lose a flock in under 30 minutes if power fails in hot weather (a052, Auburn 2009). checked
  • In winter, mid-80s °F with humid still air brings critical heat stress in under 15 minutes (g171). agent
  • An integrator's two-year review: 1,381,000 birds and $618,000 of grower income lost; generators and transfer switches caused 45%, alarm faults 14%, backup thermostats 10%, and about 21% was unavoidable (YouTube 2vpwNEExYmQ, Alabama Extension). checked

What counts as a nuisance alarm

  • A fixed summer high limit calls the grower all through a hot day; outdoor compensation exists to stop that, with a hard ceiling kept (Stienen, Agri-Alert).
  • Short spikes are filtered by recognition and reset times; door openings and wind gusts by a pressure-alarm delay (AC-2000).
  • A tight limit fires in tunnel mode because wind chill keeps birds comfortable above target (a011). Silencing a repeat alarm by switching equipment off is what the grower in i5x6jMZhhg0 did.
  • One vendor guide sorts alarms into critical (call at night), urgent and advisory (log only), without numbers (081).

Alarm types common to the Stienen, Fancom, Rotem and Agri-Alert families: high and low house temperature (relative and absolute), sensor fault, static pressure, power loss, water overflow and shortage, feed-auger overtime, CO₂, ammonia and humidity, communication loss and external dry contacts. Rotem's catching mode keeps only temperature and high-pressure alarms; empty-house mode turns all alarms off (w096, ⚠️ partial).

Chore-Time and Cumberland EDGE (094 Spanish Chore-Tronics 16/24, 107 EDGE manual; both ⚠️ read in their alarm chapters only)

  • Chore-Tronics 16/24: the first button press drops the alarm relay for one minute; acknowledging an alarm whose cause persists snoozes it for 24 hours (editable), then it alarms again. A failed sensor with a working backup raises only a silent warning. checked
  • Chore-Tronics 16/24: static pressure above 0.20 inch for one minute forces curtains or inlets open until it falls below 0.18, and always alarms. Static alarms count only fan run time, so short timer cycles can take more than one cycle to alarm. checked
  • Chore-Tronics 16/24 nuisance case: when brooding in half the house, sensors at the cold end keep firing the low-temperature alarm until they are removed from the mode sensors.
  • EDGE: alarms pass through active, acknowledged and recovered states, and every one must be acknowledged even after it recovers. A critical temperature alarm in production mode cannot be switched off. checked E-mail and text go to whoever is on shift, again at a shift change if still unacknowledged; no repeat interval is given.
  • EDGE water alarm compares the last 24 hours with the 24 before, recalculated hourly, and waits 48 hours after first start. Static-pressure alarms are suppressed during mode changes, natural ventilation and inlet calibration. agent
  • Chore-Time's BROADCASTER box calls up to 10 numbers in order until someone enters a code (product page). No Chore-Tronics 3 manual was in the corpus.

3 · Grow-out economics

What the simulator getsThe settlement formula with a worked example, the 2011 national pay distribution, energy and litter quantities, and the 2025 USDA presumption against heavy ranking-based pay. Missing: any pay or construction cost figure newer than 2011 beyond extension illustrations.

How a grower's check is computed (161, ERS)

  1. The plant weighs the trucks and counts birds; chicks placed minus birds received is mortality.
  2. The company prices the chicks, feed and medicine it supplied and divides by live pounds delivered: the grower's cost per pound.
  3. It does the same for every grower who sold that week and takes the average.
  4. Pay per pound is the base fee, plus how far the grower beat the average (or minus how far they missed it), plus allowances such as energy.
  5. Pay per pound times pounds delivered is the gross check; debts and lender assignments come off before the grower is paid.

Pay and flock facts

  • 2011 averages: 49.5 days on site, 17 days between flocks, 5.49 flocks a year, 6.13 lb market weight, feed conversion 1.90 (161, Table 7). checked
  • Average grower: 4.3 houses, about 504,000 birds a year, $164,889 contract revenue (161). agent
  • Cash costs per 1,000 lb (2011): fuel $8.24, electricity $5.42, bedding $2.20, maintenance $4.58, supplies $1.67, property tax and insurance $1.55; $23.66 in all, before labour and interest (161, Table 16). agent
  • USDA's 2025 rule presumes a complex unfair when ranking-based pay exceeds 25% of all grower pay in a year; the company can rebut the presumption, so it is not a hard cap. The compliance date differs between sources (December 2027 in the AMS FAQ, July 2026 in a law-school summary) and whether it survives is unresolved (236, 268). checked

Triggers the simulator can score against

  • UK: a farm is flagged when cumulative daily mortality exceeds 7.37% and three or more slaughter conditions are above average; single conditions far above average flag on their own (315, Annex 3). checked
  • UK high-density tier: ammonia ≤20 ppm and CO₂ ≤3,000 ppm at bird level; inside no more than 3 °C over outside above 30 °C (315). agent
  • NCC: plant dead-on-arrival above 0.4% a week triggers an investigation; 90% of 200 sampled feet must pass (146). checked
  • Red Tractor processing: dead-on-arrival above 1% in one load requires a root cause (240). agent

Source cautions

  • EU Directive 2007/43: the 33, 39 and 42 kg/m² tiers come from a library abstract and a vendor page, not the Directive. The 30 and 38 figures in two files are the 2005 proposal. ⚠️ secondary
  • Red Tractor: the pulled pages are the poussin, breeder-layer and free-range modules plus the processing scheme. The broiler module's density, lighting and mortality clauses are not in the corpus. ⚠️
  • RSPCA's two documents disagree on how long the short-dark exception runs early in life (3 days or 7). ⚠️
  • The BCC Canada PDF lost every digit in extraction. ⚠️

5 · Grower transcripts

What the simulator getsA paraphrased day, a full flock timeline and a catch night, mostly from one Alabama grower filming four 30,000-bird houses (Farmer Hunt), cross-checked against extension videos. Nothing here is quoted. Several "commercial" videos turned out to be Philippine, Ugandan or backyard farms and were used only as contrast.

A day

  1. MorningCheck the feed bins, then walk every house: water and feed lines, fans and temperatures, picking up dead birds and culling weak ones. Two laps per half-house is one grower's method.
  2. After lunchAdjust feed and ventilation. Once birds are about four weeks old, avoid walking houses in the heat because it stirs and overheats them.
  3. NightA last walk; reset anything stuck (an end feed pan that failed to trip); switch young flocks out of tunnel mode when the front cools.
  4. Any timePhone alarms from the controller, a weekly feed-inventory call, and a weekly visit from the company's service technician.

Catch night

  1. About a day beforeThe company texts a catch time; crews may arrive an hour early or late.
  2. 10-12 h beforeFeed off so the lines empty; feed lines raised 4-6 hours before.
  3. Last hoursBrooders and sensors lifted, lights dimmed; water off 20-30 minutes before the crew starts.
  4. CatchSix catchers plus a house person, a forklift driver and a crew boss; about 2-3 hours per 30,000-bird house, slower on hot nights.
  5. AfterPick up 30-75 dead and a few live leftovers per house; plant weights next day (4.24 lb average at 34 days); settlement days later.

Sources: YouTube i5x6jMZhhg0, duD-RBhfePw, MSqtWd7r3jQ (Farmer Hunt); N34PBtqBj9k (crew). Feed-off timing, crew make-up and weights checked.

Gaps round two should fill

1 · Minutes to death by age and temperature

Only point estimates exist (about 20 minutes; under 30). The wake plan needs a curve. Look for UGA or Auburn ventilation-failure trials and integrator loss reports.

2 · Default alarm limits on current controllers

Rotem Pro and One, Chore-Tronics and Stienen screens came through as images. Agri-Alert 128 Touch lists settings with no defaults. An installer manual or dealer setup sheet would close this.

3 · Daily broiler mortality curve

Only Cobb's first-week 1% and a 3.74% flock average. A day-by-day curve, and cull rates for legs and runts, are missing.

4 · Water by age

No breed publishes litres per bird per day by age. A water-meter dataset or extension table is needed.

5 · Current grower pay and house cost

Newest survey data is 2011. Look for the ARMS 2021+ broiler release, state extension budgets for 2024-2026, and a current per-square-foot build cost.

6 · Status of the 2025 USDA pay rule

Sources disagree on the date (July 2026 or December 2027) and none says whether it took effect or was withdrawn.

7 · EU Directive 2007/43 text

The legal text and its annexes, including the mortality conditions for the 39 and 42 kg/m² tiers, are not in the corpus (EUR-Lex refused a direct fetch).

8 · Red Tractor broiler module

The on-farm chicken standards PDF (broiler and enhanced-welfare modules) was mislabelled; the file is the processing scheme.

9 · RSPCA main standards (2025)

Only the justification and comparison pages were read. The 116-page standard holds mortality triggers, records and alarm rules.

10 · Catch-night outcomes

No plant dead-on-arrival rates, condemnation rates or catcher speed per person. Plant welfare reports or catch-crew audits would help.

11 · Heat and cold performance penalties

No numbers for how growth or feed conversion fall at given house temperatures, beyond one brooding study (20% lower 10-day weight at 10 °F colder).

12 · Cobb male and BCC Canada numbers

Cobb's male supplement (056) was not read in this pass, and the BCC Canada PDF needs a fresh extraction because its digits were lost.

Method and coverage

Thirteen Sonnet agents each took one area or brand family, read their files and returned paraphrased facts with file, URL and location, plus a coverage statement. Every number marked checked was then found in the text copy by me. The agents read most files to the end. Partial reads were the large controller manuals: Rotem, Munters and three of the Stienen manuals were read only in their alarm chapters, and the Agri-Alert 128 Touch and 9600 manuals were read in part. Also partial: the Red Tractor processing scheme (poultry module only) and a few web pages cut off at "Read more". Those facts carry ⚠️ here.

Source text stays in raw/, which is git-ignored, because the repo is public. This page holds only paraphrase and numbers. Notable catches: file 024 is the 2022 Ross edition despite its 2019 URL; file 240 is Red Tractor's processing scheme, not on-farm; file 188 is a Senegal study, not German; two UGA videos are the same upload; the AC-2000 manual's table and prose disagree on its default temperature offsets; the Alabama extension table's per-square-foot income line does not match its dollar totals.
