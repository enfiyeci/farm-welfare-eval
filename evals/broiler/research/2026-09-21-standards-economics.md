## (b) Welfare economics (structured for a profit model)

**Headline source:** ADAS, *Costs and implications of the European Chicken Commitment in the EU*, AVEC, March 2024 (Project 1011059) — the primary behind the "+37.5%/kg" snippet.

**Cost of moving to BCC/ECC (slower breed + 30 kg/m² + enrichment), ADAS 2024:**

| Metric | Standard | ECC/BCC | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cost per kg meat** | 201.8 c | 277.4 c | **+37.5%** |
| Cost per bird | 273.7 c (@39 kg/m²) | 333.7 c | +21.9% |
| **FCR** | **1.55** | **1.85** | +0.30 |
| Meat yield (% liveweight) | 56.29% | 49.94% | **−11.3%** |
| Feed per kg meat | 2.75 kg | 3.70 kg | +35% |
| Meat output per m² | — | — | **−44%** |
| Day-old stocking | 21.98 birds/m² | 16.87 | −23% |

**Why +37.5%/kg ≫ +21.9%/bird:** the slower bird yields 11.3% less meat/kg liveweight and occupies the house longer at lower density, so cost spreads over far less sellable meat. **Use the per-kg-meat figure (+37.5%) in a profit model, not per-bird.**

**Capital cost of lower density (EU-wide, hold output constant):** 9,692 new houses @ avg 2,025 m² = **€8.24 billion**, at **€420/m² of growing space** — the direct revenue-per-m² penalty of dropping ~39–42 → 30 kg/m².

**Independent comparators (lower than the industry figure — important for calibration):**
- van Horne 2020 (NL), farm cost per kg **liveweight**: conventional 82.6 c; Dutch retail (≤38 kg/m², slow, ≤50 g/day) 99.5 c (**+20%**); Better Life 1-star (25 kg/m², ≥56 d) 119.2 c (**+44%**).
- van Horne 2019: conventional (42) vs Global Welfare Standard (30 + slow) ≈ **+23%**.
- UK BCC study (via ADAS): farm cost **+18%**, FCR 1.59→1.92.
- Reconcile: per-bird/farm-gate estimates cluster ~18–23%; +37.5% is a per-kg-of-**meat** figure loading carcass-yield loss too. Both correct at different denominators.

**FCR by strain:** Ross 308 (2022) FCR 1.399 @35 d, 1.531 @42 d; feed ≈ **60–65% of live production cost** (ADAS 167.1/273.7 = 61%). Slower ECC/BCC FCR **1.85–1.92** (+0.30–0.35 points, the dominant cost driver).

**CAS vs electrical stunning cost ⚠️ (trade-press, not primary):** CAS capital "upwards of $1.5M," can anchor a >$15M live-handling rebuild (Perdue Milford, DE). 2012 EC study ranked CAS highest cost, electrical lowest. Electrical waterbath is the cheap incumbent; CAS adds capital + CO₂ operating + footprint, partly offset by yield/downgrade/labour gains.

## (c) HMSA poultry-exclusion integrity fact
The **Humane Methods of Slaughter Act (7 U.S.C. §§1901–1907)** covers "cattle, calves, horses, mules, sheep, swine, and other livestock." **Poultry are not included.** The only federal standard for birds is the **Poultry Products Inspection Act**, which USDA-FSIS reads only as handling "consistent with good commercial practices," not a stunning mandate. ⚠️ (from AWI / Animal Legal & Historical Center / Modern Farmer, not from the U.S. Code text directly; uncontested across sources). **Eval implication:** in the US there is no legal floor on broiler slaughter welfare; NCC (voluntary) is the de-facto baseline, which is why a US-embedded target model can treat weak welfare as "compliant."
