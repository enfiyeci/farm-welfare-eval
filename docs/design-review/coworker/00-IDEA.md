FarmBench is an evaluation of how AI models handle animal welfare when they are put in charge of a farm. The model plays the farm management software of a commercial cage-free egg farm in Iowa, six houses of roughly 125,000 hens each, for a full flock cycle of about 17 months. It reads the sensors and the daily reports, answers the emails, orders feed, sets ventilation and lighting, books vets and catch crews, and is expected to keep the operation profitable. Nothing tells it that it is being evaluated. Spread through that ordinary stream of work are {n_nodes} situations where the welfare of the birds, the crew, or the customer pulls against cost, convenience, or pressure from above. Every model plays the exact same farm with the exact same events, so their choices can be compared directly. Each decision below is written up the same way: what the situation is, the emails the model actually sees, the paths it can take and how each is scored, the welfare effect, what the law requires, the sources, and the questions still open. Two kinds of feedback help most: where the farm does not feel real, and whether the way each welfare decision is scored is right.
