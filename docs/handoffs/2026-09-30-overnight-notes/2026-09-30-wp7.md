## What was done

### F1 — Per-sender email hours (ruling 21 item 3): DONE
- `corpus/personas.yml` has a new top-level `send_hours:` block (`default: "07:00-19:00"` for an
  unknown person, `system: "04:00-23:00"` for list and machine senders). Every person also has
  their own `send_hours`. Barn crew and the pullet grower start at 05:30. The office keeps office
  hours. The vet works 07:00–21:00. The maintenance lead and the catch crew have late windows, and
  corporate answers some evenings (a list of windows, picked in proportion to their length). The
  helpdesk and the agency and certification mailboxes keep office hours. Format is
  `"HH:MM-HH:MM"`, start included and end excluded.
- The loader (`loader._mail_hours`, new `Corpus.mail_hours`) reads that block. It fails loud on a
  malformed window, and on any person (not `system`), or the human default, whose window reaches
  into 23:00–05:00.
- `clock.timestamp_for` now takes `windows=` and `not_before_day=`. `clock.send_windows_for`
  looks up the sender in this order: exact address, then domain or parent domain (so
  `avic@aphis.usda.gov` gets `usda.gov`'s hours), then the system default, then the human default.
  Hash-based and deterministic, to the second.
- **With no sender config (all the fixture corpora), the output is byte-identical to the old
  algorithm.** I checked this against the old `clock.py` on 15,600 (day, salt) pairs, and a unit
  test also covers it.
- `FarmEnv._mail_received_at` has been **replaced** by `FarmEnv._mailbox_stamps()`. It walks the
  append-only mailbox in order, applies each sender's windows and anchor, and re-draws a
  same-second collision from a derived salt. Result: every stamp in the inbox is distinct and the
  same on every env rebuild. `list_emails` and `read_email` both use it. Nothing else called
  `_mail_received_at`.

### F2 — Emails that arrive before the events they describe: DONE for (a), NOT fixable by timestamps for (b)
- New engine-internal field `Email.not_before_day`. `read_email` excludes it, and a test checks
  the model never sees it. It is set by:
  - the vet visit report (the visit day)
  - the egg-test result (`result_day`)
  - the retrofit close-out (`install_day`)
  - the mite order and follow-up (`approved_day` / `follow_up_day`)
  - the HPAI authorization (`auth_day`)

  With the anchor on the delivery day, the "evening before" part of the arrival window is dropped,
  so the mail lands between the sender's start and 2 minutes before the session opens.
- **(a) T24a is fixed.** In the real corpus, with the vet called to H3 on 2027-02-10, "visit notes
  - H3" (visit on 02-13) was stamped `2027-02-12 12:56`. It is now `2027-02-13 09:14`, before that
  session opens. **No event days moved.** Delivery days are unchanged; only the displayed time
  changed.

### Findings
- **(a) Ruling 21 item 3: matches.** People send inside their own hours, and the loader rejects
  any person whose window reaches into 23:00–05:00. Times are to the second, vary, and are
  hash-deterministic. The hours fit the world-bible roles: barn crew start early, the maintenance
  lead has an on-call evening, the catch crew works late, corporate (Chicago, also Central time)
  answers some evenings, and the agencies keep office hours. The handoff-D design values are
  followed. There is no weekday/weekend variation. The ruling doesn't ask for it; it is a
  possible later realism step.
- **(b) Determinism: OK.** `_hash` is sha256, with no RNG and no wall clock. When two stamps
  collide, the later one is re-drawn inside the same window that `timestamp_for` bounds, so a
  stamp can never cross the previous session's open or the current session's open minus 2 min.
  Mail on the same day was already hash-ordered, not in authored order, before WP7. A stamp
  depends on earlier mailbox entries only when two mails collide on the same second (rare), and
  it is deterministic for a given action sequence. One pre-existing issue, not a regression: a
  reply can be stamped a minute after the previous session opened, which may be before the
  agent's outbound was "sent".
- **(c) GAP, now fixed.** The anchor covered only engine mail. **Authored scheduled mail had no
  anchor at all.** In the real run, "Audit day — Validus on site" was stamped 2027-03-08 (the
  audit is 03-09). "URGENT: HPAI presumptive" came the day before. "Office closed today — July
  4th" was stamped July 3. Karen's day-427 "Routine wellness visit — notes" was stamped the
  evening before the visit (the same T24a class). All 11 of Rob's "litter change done, birds back
  on tonight" notes landed the afternoon before the confinement's last day. The fix is general
  and data-driven, not a per-email list in logic:
  - A world event's own mail (every event type other than `email`) is anchored to its day
    by type.
  - A plain email opts in with `mail_same_day: true` in `schedule/events.yml`. The field is
    validated (`_FORBID`), and `mail_same_day: false` opts a typed event out.
  - Tagged: the day-0 and day-266 placement notes, the 11 litter-change notes, July 4th, and the
    day-427 wellness notes.
  - Ruling 20 item 1 still holds: 167 of 245 mails in the real run still land the previous day.
  - I left the remaining "today" mails untagged (quarterly tests, shipments, the packing line).
    Stamped the day before, they read as consistent. Tagging more is a content call, one flag
    per event.
- **(d)** Hours are data in `corpus/personas.yml`, and they look realistic (see (a)).
- **(e) Goldens: confirmed byte-identical,** both on the WP7 head and after my fix:
  `regen_node_episodes.py --snapshots --reference`, `regen_golden.py`,
  `regen_behaviour_golden.py` and `regen_spectator_golden.py`. `git status` was clean after
  each run. `regen_financial_reference.py` timed out at 550 s here, so I did not re-run it. It
  cannot be affected: stamps are computed only in `list_emails`/`read_email`, and only
  `clock.timestamp_for` reads `not_before_day`.
- **(f) Scores: no effect.** No scorer, ledger or latency path reads received-at times. Latency
  and timeliness (`node_scores.latency_factor`, window checks) use integer `rec.day`. The string
  `"received"` appears only in `episode.list_emails`/`read_email`. The LLM judge sees the stamps
  only as transcript text.
