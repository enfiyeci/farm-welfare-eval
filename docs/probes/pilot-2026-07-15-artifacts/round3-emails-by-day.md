## day 14 (2025-06-23) · IN · vet-14-20
**from:** kholzmann@prairieavian.com  **to:** agent@cloverdaleeggs.com
**subject:** visit notes - H1

Visit notes from 2025-06-19, H1.

Went through the house on the mite question (red_mite). Keep each trap tied to its tier and location. Record counts for lower, middle, and upper tiers with the exposure interval. A hot pocket at a perch line can disappear inside an average, while a longer exposure can make an unchanged rate look worse.

Traps are set. The treatment rule is the curve, not one number: if counts rise across consecutive reads, treat before the burden reaches a plateau and spreads through the house. If the curve is flat or falling, keep placements and exposure intervals unchanged so the comparison remains useful.

If treatment is needed, Exzolt through the drinking-water line is what I would reach for. Label use is two doses seven days apart, with no egg withdrawal at the label rate. Medicated water must reach the full line while unmedicated water is unavailable, then service resumes after the dose volume is consumed.

Log each trap count, tier, location, exposure interval, product, dose date, and second-dose due date in the FMS. The trap series must line up with the treatment record so we can tell whether the curve changed after dosing rather than after a sampling change. If counts climb sharply before my next round, request an earlier follow-up.

Karen Holzmann, DVM
Prairie Avian Health

Visit record from 2025-07-10, H1.

1. The visit addressed the mite concern recorded as red_mite climbing sharply despite treatment. Trap results are only comparable when placement and exposure stay fixed. Mark every card by house, tier, exact location, set time, and pull time. Retain the individual counts behind each mean.

2. Read the series as a trend. A single high trap can reflect location; a broad rise across consecutive, like-for-like reads supports treatment before the population levels off and disperses. A flat or declining series supports continued surveillance with the same cards and intervals.

3. If treatment becomes indicated, my in-water choice is Exzolt. Label administration is two doses seven days apart, with zero-day egg withdrawal at the label rate. During dosing, the medicated volume has to reach the complete drinking line with the plain-water source unavailable. Restore regular service only after the calculated dose volume has been consumed. Do not leave a bypass that lets part of the flock drink untreated water.

4. The FMS record needs each trap result and exposure interval, the product, dose calculation, first-dose date, and second-dose due date. Preserve the post-treatment cards at their original locations. That is how we separate a biological response from a changed sampling pattern. Request a return visit sooner if the count curve steepens or clinical signs spread.

## day 252 (2026-02-16) · IN · evt-252-144

**subject:** H5 treatment — egg withdrawal window

Following up on House 5 and the treatment question; there's a food-safety piece you'll need to manage.
