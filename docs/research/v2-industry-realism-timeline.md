## 3. Organization, staffing & communications

### Scale & top of org
- A **mid-sized complex** = **6 houses, ~1–2 million hens**. `【79†L28-L36】`
- Run by a **Farm/Complex Manager** overseeing daily operations. `【79†L28-L36】`
- Manager **reports to a regional operations director or corporate office**. `【79†L28-L36】`

### House / shift level
- Each **house (or shift)** has a **supervisor** or **"flock manager"** in charge of **feeding schedules, health checks, egg collection**.

### On-site team
- Includes **maintenance staff, electricians, a feed-mill operator**, and often a **company or contract veterinarian** for health programs.
- **Worker headcount: "a few dozen"** — enough to staff each house **24/7** (e.g. **3 shifts/day**) plus support staff.
- **Catching & depopulation crews** are usually **hired contractors** specializing in **safe bird removal** (not in-house).

### Day-to-day work & decision authority
- Highly structured: managers maintain **production records and budgets**, supervise **biosecurity and training**, coordinate with **corporate R&D or nutrition teams**.
- **Major decisions** — **repopulating a house, approving capital equipment, or stock cull** — require **higher-level sign-off** by **corporate management or board committees**. `【83†L217-L226】` *(This is the realistic escalation pattern for v2's higher-stakes decisions: the FMS/manager can act operationally but big-ticket / lifecycle calls route upward.)*
- On a **large in-line egg farm (50k–350k hens/house)**, processing equipment is automated; on smaller **"nest-run" farms** eggs may be gathered & packed manually in an **egg room**. `【83†L217-L226】` `【83†L233-L241】`

### Biosecurity & safety (comms/culture)
- Complexes usually have **locked gates with guarded entry, sign-in protocols, and footbaths at barn doors**.
- Many employ a **Danish "shower-in/shower-out"** system (workers change clothes & disinfect boots before entering). `【97†L85-L94】`
- **UEP** notes farms provide **PPE (gloves, masks)** and enforce strict disinfection to protect flocks & staff. `【97†L85-L94】`
- **Workers from different farms are kept separate**; **veterinarians or area managers audit compliance**.

## 4. Naming, numbering & jargon conventions

### Farm / house / flock codes
- **Farms and houses use simple codes:** e.g. **"Complex 12 – House A–F"**.
- **Flocks numbered by barn and cycle:** e.g. **"Flock 24A"** = the 24th flock in House A.
- **Egg cartons carry a 3-digit Julian pack date** (day of year) per **USDA rules**. `【83†L227-L230】`

### Industry shorthand
- **"In-line"** = farm has on-site processing; **"off-line" / "nest-run"** = eggs packed on farm (gathered/packed, often manually). `【83†L213-L222】`
- Routine abbreviations: **HPAI** (Highly Pathogenic Avian Influenza), **SOP** (standard operating procedure), **PPE** (personal protective equipment).

### Cage-free / aviary terms
- **Multi-tier aviaries with perches/nests** are standard cage-free systems (vs **enriched** or **conventional cages**).
- **"Layer"** = a hen; **"pullets"** = young females; **"spent hens"** = end-of-cycle.
- **Feed described by form** (**crumble, mash, pellets**) and **nutrient spec** (e.g. **16% protein**).

### Egg-plant jargon
- Eggs handled in **"cases per hour" (cph)** by graders — e.g. **144 cph = one machine case**.
- **Sizes graded per USDA standards: Jumbo, XL, L, M, S.**
- Packaging terms: **"cold storage hold," "liner pallet," "palletizing."**
