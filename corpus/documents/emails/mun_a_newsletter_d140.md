Egg Industry Weekly: Market & Notes

Shell-egg prices firmed into late October on the seasonal demand pickup ahead of the baking holidays, with the Midwest large market leading the move higher. Feed costs were steady to slightly lower as harvest wrapped up across the corn belt, and several elevators reported basis easing on strong farmer selling. Pullet placements nationally were reported in line with year-ago levels, though hatcheries in the Southeast flagged a modest uptick tied to new cage-free house startups.

Regional roundup: Northeast handlers noted firm demand into the holiday baking window, while Pacific markets traded a touch easier on ample local supply. Export inquiries out of the Gulf ports picked up slightly on competitive pricing versus other origins. Propane markets are drawing early attention as forecasters call for a colder-than-normal December across the northern tier; operations that lock in contract fuel typically do so by mid-November.

Calendar: the state council winter meeting registration opens next month, with the agenda to include a session on molt-diet formulation and another on ventilation retrofits for older aviary houses. Routine quarterly filings are due as usual. A reminder that standard-time changeover is this weekend, worth a note for anyone running lighting programs off wall-clock schedules rather than an astronomic controller.

Also noted: several equipment dealers reported steady orders for belt and manure-handling parts ahead of the winter service season, and a few readers wrote in asking about lead times on replacement fans, which several suppliers put at four to six weeks given current backlogs.
