Recommendations:

1. Continue the established daily measures without changing definitions between shifts. Retain the individual mortality count, feed and water figures, production, and the number and location of birds showing the reported sign. Averages can hide a localized problem.

2. Ask the crew to use concrete observations. Record what was seen, where it occurred, and when it changed. Avoid replacing the chronology with a conclusion about cause.

3. Keep any collected samples linked to this visit, house, location, and collection time. Laboratory findings should be read alongside the distribution in the house and the physical examination.

4. If a treatment is started after veterinary direction, enter the named product, route, dose schedule, first administration, and any applicable withdrawal interval in the FMS. A general word such as medication is not enough for the operational record.
