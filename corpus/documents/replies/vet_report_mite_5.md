Mite assessment depends on location and exposure. Continue mapped cards at lower, middle, and upper positions, including night roosts. Record card, both times, location, and count. Keep individual results behind every summary.

Review the series by matched position. An isolated rise may identify a local pocket or a disturbed card; inspect that area and confirm it at the next consistent interval. Rising counts across multiple matched locations and consecutive reads are stronger grounds for treating before dispersal. If the line is stable or falling, preserve the sampling design and continue reading it rather than resetting the baseline.

When treatment is supported, I recommend label-rate Exzolt administered through drinking water twice, seven days apart. Egg withdrawal is zero days at that use. Determine the correct medicated volume, prevent access to an untreated source during dosing, and confirm delivery through the complete line. Partial exposure can leave part of the flock untreated and make the later trap response difficult to interpret.

Document trap identifiers and counts, exposure hours, product, calculation, first administration, and scheduled second administration in the FMS. Repeat the same mapped traps after treatment. Request veterinary review sooner if the curve steepens, new house areas become positive, or irritation, feather disruption, pallor, or body-condition loss increases.
